Chapter 3

Deadpan, Dead Body

The heavy smell of sweat and iron filled the home gym.

Arnav’s body was lying flat on the floor near the bench press — face pale, eyes wide open, unmoving.

The barbell lay toppled awkwardly near his head.

Veer Khandelwal stood nearby, hands in his pockets, surveying the scene with a blank face.

Mehar leaned against the wall, arms crossed.

Two uniformed cops quietly moved around the room, taping off the area.

Paras and Satyam sat together on a nearby bench, whispering.

Mehar glanced at Paras and Satyam, then asked Veer,

"What are these two doing here?"

Paras immediately stood up, grinning like an idiot.

"I want a dead body," he said cheerfully.

Mehar frowned.

"Why do you want a dead body?"

Paras shrugged innocently.

"Why don't you want one?"

Veer ignored them, stepping closer to Arnav’s body. He crouched, observing the position of the barbell, the marks on the neck, the blood pooling oddly at the ear.

Satyam hovered behind Veer, twitchy.

"Uh, sir... shouldn’t you... Call the forensic team or something?"

Veer didn’t even look up.

"Satyam, do me a favour. Stop breathing down my neck."

Satyam stepped back awkwardly, wiping his palms on his jeans.

Mehar moved closer to Veer, lowering her voice.

"Good that he's dead," she said, voice sharp.

Veer raised an eyebrow without looking up.

"You know, saying that out loud makes you a suspect, right?"

Mehar smirked slightly.

"He once hit me. A long time ago. Forgive me if I don’t light a candle for him."

Veer shook his head, muttering,

"Such emotional people I work with."

He carefully circled the equipment. His eyes scanned the dust, the weights, and the tiny patterns on the gym mat.

He crouched again, lifting a stray gym towel with a pen. Blood — small smudges — under it.

Satyam peered over his shoulder.

"Sir, do you think... maybe it’s an accident?"

Veer turned slowly toward him.

"When was the last time you accidentally dropped a 150 kilo barbell on your throat while perfectly re-racking it?"

Satyam opened his mouth, then shut it.

Veer stood up, stretching.

He tapped Mehar’s shoulder lightly.

"Call the forensic boys. Tell them to be fast."

As Mehar made the call, Satyam whispered loudly to Paras,

"I told you something’s wrong! We are next, I swear!"

Paras slapped the back of Satyam’s head lightly.

"Will you shut up, you’re scaring yourself."

Veer watched them both and muttered under his breath,

"If someone doesn’t kill them, I might."

The investigation continued — and every time Veer leaned down to examine something, Satyam nervously asked a question or jumped at a sound.

It was getting on Veer's nerves.

Finally, Veer sighed.

"Anyone interrupts again, they’re carrying the body to the morgue."

Everyone went silent.

Outside Arnav’s house, the night air was thick and heavy.

Satyam, still jumpy from the investigation, hurriedly dialled Kritika.

She picked up after a few rings.

"Kritika... Arnav is dead," Satyam said in a low voice.

A brief pause.

Then her voice, calm and composed:

"What? Anyway, tell me the full story — how did he die, who did it — but in the morning. I want every detail."

Satyam frowned, confused.

"Morning? You’re not even curious now?"

In the background, he caught a muffled voice — light, teasing.

"Baby, come back here," came Abhay's unmistakable tone.

Satyam blinked.

"Wait. Are you... With Abhay? This late at night? What are you two even—"

Before he could finish, Paras yanked the phone from his hand.

"ABHAY, YOU SHIRTLESS PEACOCK!" Paras shouted into the speaker. "I swear, I’ll twist those gorgeous six-packs of yours into a modern art sculpture! Don't think your muscle show will save you!"

Satyam tried to snatch the phone back, but Paras danced out of reach, grinning wickedly.

Kritika’s tired voice floated through.

"Paras, stop being a moron. It’s a full-time job for you now?"

Satyam finally managed to grab the phone, huffing. He immediately put it on speaker.

"Kritika, seriously... someone’s after us. Arnav is dead. What if we’re next?"

Kritika sighed, her tone business-like.

"Call Manav. He always has some ridiculous genius plan."

Satyam groaned.

"He must be busy… probably stuck with his… uh… cheerleading squad again."

Kritika chuckled dryly.

"So am I, in case you missed it."

Before Satyam could argue, Abhay’s voice boomed again from Kritika’s side.

"Come on, baby! One more round! Pleaseee!"

Satyam froze, his face twisting in disgust. Paras burst out laughing.

Without another word, Satyam cut the call and shoved his phone deep into his pocket.

Kritika’s Apartment

Abhay lounges lazily on the bed, half-covered, catching his breath. Kritika sits at her desk, lit by her laptop screen, legs casually crossed, hair in a loose bun. She's barely dressed, but entirely composed.

ABHAY

(grinning)

These thighs should be illegal. And that waist? It's like temptation to do yoga.

KRITIKA

(not looking at him)

Abhay, you're not here for poetry night. Breathe quietly. You’re disrupting my process.

ABHAY

(half-joking)

It’s tough when the hot professor assigns homework in lingerie.

KRITIKA

(turning slightly)

One more line like that, and I’ll assign you burpees till sunrise.

She tosses him a resistance band from her drawer.

KRITIKA

Stretch. Cool down. And stop pretending your hormones are my problem.

ABHAY

(smirking)

You always get like this when you're scheming something. Who’s the unlucky one this time?

KRITIKA

(smiling slightly, eyes on screen)

That’s classified. But let’s just say—planning saves a lot of cleanup.

ABHAY

You’re scary, you know that?

KRITIKA

Only to the slow ones.

She clicks through her tabs—spreadsheets, encrypted folders, a map. Abhay watches her, then mutters:

ABHAY

Just sayin’. Your brain turns me on more than your body. And your body’s... unfair.

KRITIKA

(glancing back, calmly)

Then be grateful. Now shut up and stretch. I’m working.

The atmosphere is tense. Arnav’s body has been taken. Satyam and Paras sit on the couch, fidgeting. Mehar leans against the wall. Veer enters the room slowly, flipping through a small notepad, unfazed.

VEER

(sipping tea from someone else’s cup)

Your friend didn’t fall off a treadmill. He was killed.

PARAS

(deadpan)

Please tell me it was a ghost. I’ve always wanted to punch one.

VEER

(to everyone)

It was the house help. Raghu.

SATYAM

(jumps up)

What?! How can you be so sure?

VEER

(looks at him like he’s a child asking why the sky is blue)

Because I’m paid to be.

SATYAM

I’m a lawyer. You can’t just make accusations like that without proof. We have the right to know what you found.

VEER

(smirking)

Lawyer, huh? Alright, Mr. Article 21. You want reasons, here they are—

(ticking on fingers)

No forced entry. The main gate camera shows no visitors.

Protein drink bottle was empty—he drank it.

Treadmill was at walking speed.

And the poison bottle? Found on the lawn.

Guess who made the drink?

MEHAR

Raghu. The only staff in the house.

(smiling wryly)

Maybe Arnav didn’t tip well.

PARAS

(grinning)

Well, if that’s the standard, remind me to tip the guy who parks my car before I get parked in a coffin.

MEHAR

(deadpan)

You tip? That’s the most surprising part of this investigation.

PARAS

(mock wounded)

I’m generous. Emotionally. Financially is... negotiable.

SATYAM

(still rattled)

But why would Raghu do it?

VEER

(puts on his coat)

That’s what I’ll find out. For now, he’s in custody.

And silence isn’t innocence. Especially when the timing is perfect.

Veer walks off, with Mehar following. Paras whistles low.

PARAS

Man, Death by protein shake. CrossFit’s finally fighting back.

Back at the station

Interior – Dim interrogation room. A table. One chair on each side. Raghu sits tense. The fan overhead groans softly. Veer enters silently, holding a black bag.

VEER

You know what this is?

He tosses the bag on the table. It lands with a flat thud. Raghu stiffens.

VEER

Ten lakh rupees. Found in your room.

Last I checked, mop duty doesn’t pay that well.

So, who gave it to you?

Raghu remains still, lips pressed tight.

VEER

You poisoned Arnav. No blood. No bruises. Just a treadmill fall and an empty protein bottle.

Neat job. A little too neat.

Now speak.

Raghu stares down. Silent. Veer waits. No response. After a beat, Veer straightens, nods slowly, and walks out of the room.

Exterior – Hallway outside the interrogation room. Mehar stands, sipping chai, watching Veer approach.

MEHAR

That angry-silent walk? Ten on ten.

You keep this up, and I’ll ask you out.

VEER

(sarcastic)

You just did.

MEHAR

(flippant)

Alright, fine. Tomorrow night. Sandwich, cold coffee, you pretending to care.

Deal?

VEER

(flat)

Only if he talks.

MEHAR

Oh, now that’s pressure. Ugh. Fine.

Interior – Interrogation room, moments later. Mehar walks in and plops down across from Raghu.

MEHAR

Heyyy Raghu ji. Tough room, huh?

Raghu avoids eye contact.

MEHAR

You know this room? It’s kind of haunted.

Last guy Veer questioned here? Gone. And the one before him. And the one before that.

She leans in just a little.

MEHAR

People say their ghosts still wander around.

I mean, between us… Veer looks hot when he’s interrogating. But he looks hotter when he's, you know… finishing the job.

Raghu shifts, confused. Clearly not used to this approach.

MEHAR

But honestly, you don’t seem like the dying type. More like… the crying type.

So just tell me what happened, hmm? I’ll make sure no one shouts at you.

Raghu gulps. Fidgets. Then, finally, cracks, voice low.

RAGHU

I… I’ll speak. I’ll tell everything.

Mehar hides a grin, stands, and walks to the door. Opens it slightly, peeking out at Veer.

MEHAR

Moonlight Café tomorrow night. Just you and me.

Veer doesn’t smile. Just nods, once.

Veer steps back inside, calm but blank-faced. Raghu looks terrified.

RAGHU

Don’t kill me, sir! I’ll tell everything! Please!

VEER

What happened now?

RAGHU

I don’t want to be a ghost, sir. Wandering in this room, like the others.

Veer pauses, processing. His expression doesn’t change, but there’s a flicker of amusement.

VEER

(low, to himself)

Mehar… you’ve made me a serial killer. My next victim is you.

He pulls a chair and sits opposite Raghu.

VEER

Start talking.

RAGHU

Three days ago, I got a WhatsApp call from an unknown number. The voice… it wasn’t human. Not a man. Not a woman. Just… mechanical. Like a machine.

Veer listens, silent and still.

RAGHU

It said, “If you want your son to study in a good school, do as I say. Kill Arnav. I will give you ten lakh.”

I hesitated. But… I needed the money for my kid. So I said yes.

Raghu wipes sweat from his forehead. Veer stays stone-faced.

RAGHU

Then the voice told me about a secret locker in Arnav Sir’s gym. Said the code out loud like it knew everything.

Next day, I opened the locker. Inside—cash. Ten lakhs.

VEER

Just like that?

RAGHU

Yes, sir. And the day after, in the vegetable market, someone came—head covered, full burqa. No talking. Just handed me a sealed envelope and left.

VEER

What was in it?

RAGHU

Typed letter. Said what poison to buy. When and how to give it. Strategy was simple—Arnav sir always took a protein shake from me before workout. I mixed the poison in it.

VEER

You never saw the person’s face?

RAGHU

No, sir. Not even for a second. No voice. Just dropped the envelope and walked away.

Veer gets up quietly. Walks out.

A junior cop comes rushing up to Veer, eager.

JUNIOR COP

Sir! That number—I'll get it traced right now. Call logs, towers, IP address—everything.

VEER

(flat)

Dumb junior. I'm going to sleep. Haven’t slept in two days. You try that instead of chasing fairy tales.

He walks off. The junior cop turns to Mehar, lost.

JUNIOR COP

Ma’am… what just happened?

MEHAR

(sips tea)

A person spending ten lakhs isn’t dumb enough to use a real number. Of course, it was a burner phone.

JUNIOR COP

Umm… Ma’am, what should we do with Raghu now?

MEHAR

He killed a man. That man deserved it, sure—he once hit me. But still… prison.

She takes another sip of her tea. Calm, casual.

MEHAR

Arrange for his custody. And give that ten lakh to Raghu’s family. Let the kid actually study.

JUNIOR COP

(stunned)

You’re serious?

MEHAR

Completely. The killer got paid, the child gets tuition, and the ghost room gets one less applicant.

The junior is still confused, scribbling notes.

JUNIOR COP

Uhh… Ma’am… Mind telling me how Arnav hit you?

MEHAR

Oh, that? It was at the party. Full house. Music blasting. Someone pushed him while dancing—he just fell on me.

JUNIOR COP

(shocked)

Fell on you!?

MEHAR

Yeah. Then he looked at me… said “Sorry”... and walked away like he did nothing.

JUNIOR COP

(flat)

That’s your definition of a hit?

MEHAR

Of course. Emotional damage counts, too, okay?

She walks away, hair flip and all. The junior stands frozen, utterly confused.
