Chapter 6

White Shirt, Dark Water

In the Evening,

Kritika sat cross-legged on her bed, laptop balanced neatly in front of her. She was wearing a plain white shirt—neither too loose nor too snug, just the right fit—and nothing underneath to cover her legs. The soft glow of the screen lit up her otherwise dim room, papers and half-finished coffee mugs scattered around like the remains of long, restless nights.

Her fingers flew across the keyboard until suddenly, they stopped.

Something on the screen had caught her eye. A detail. A connection. A truth. She leaned in, squinting slightly. Whatever she had discovered, it didn’t show on her face—no smirk, no shock—only that familiar, unreadable calm that came when she was in control.

No reaction crossed her face, but her fingers stilled. She exhaled once, reached for her phone, and dialed Manav.

“I know who hired the hitman,” she said, flatly.

“What?” Manav’s voice shot through. “Whoever it is, they’ve messed with the wrong people. After Paras—”

“Relax,” Kritika cut in. “I’m not telling you over the phone. You’ve been dumb enough to keep your line tappable since 2019. I’ll talk tomorrow.”

Without waiting for a reply, she hung up and dialed again.

“Abhay,” she said, voice crisp. “Get your ass over here. Now.”

“Kritika, I… I’m kind of in the middle of—”

“NOW,” she snapped. “And don’t forget the condom, you moron.”

She tossed the phone aside and closed her laptop, eyes already set on what she wanted next.

Abhay stepped inside, his chest rising as he caught his breath — a mix of excitement and nerves dancing on his face. And then he saw her.

Kritika sat on the edge of her bed, wearing nothing but a crisp white shirt — simple, clean, yet something about the way it draped over her made it impossible to look away. His eyes widened for a fraction of a second, the breath he had just caught slipping away again.

KRITIKA

“Close the door,” she said coolly, “or are you waiting for applause?”

Snapped back into the moment, Abhay fumbled behind him and shut the door.

He stepped forward, slowly reaching out to hold her — but she didn’t wait. With sharp intent, she grabbed his wrist and dragged him toward the living room, her grip firm, her pace brisk, as if she were leading a child who had disobeyed.

With one hand on his chest, she pushed him down onto the sofa. He dropped onto it, caught between awe and anticipation.

Kritika didn’t say a word.

She climbed onto his lap and leaned in, her lips pressing against his in a deep, slow kiss — the kind that didn’t ask for permission or forgiveness. Her fingers found their place behind his neck, pulling him closer, as the kiss deepened — deliberate, charged, and full of silent intent.

He smiled against her lips, slightly breathless.

ABHAY

“Why the rush, baby?” he asked, his hands sliding along her waist.

KRITIKA

“Make it quick,” she muttered, her voice sharp but low. “I’m not free like you. And don’t baby me.”

That was the only warning he got before she silenced him again — not with words, but with action.

No more talk. Just heat, urgency, and two tangled bodies chasing something unsaid. The room didn’t need music; the rhythm found its own beat.

A night long for them — but not for us.

Later, silence.

They lay next to each other, barely touching. The air between them was warm but distant.

Abhay turned to her, a weak grin barely forming.

ABHAY

“Why don’t you ever let me call you baby? Aren’t you my girlfriend, at least in theory?”

KRITIKA

“I don’t date. You’re fun. Like a toy.”

She shrugged as if she didn’t expect a reaction.

Abhay didn’t give her one. He watched the ceiling, lips slightly parted, but said nothing.

Kritika stood, unbothered by the sheets slipping off her. She walked to the bathroom without a glance back.

On the bed, Abhay pulled a pillow over his face.

ABHAY

“Yeah… I’m nothing. I’m going to sleep. Too tired for this.”

The bathroom door shut. The water started.

Abhay closed his eyes.

The building was asleep, but the night wasn’t.

Soft footsteps echoed in the hallway — steady, confident, almost too quiet for this hour. A woman emerged from the shadows, hood drawn low, her presence sharp enough to cut through the silence. She stopped in front of Kritika’s flat.

Then — nothing.

She just stood there.

Head slightly tilted, as if listening. Thinking.

Like she was weighing something.

Then, slowly, deliberately, she pulled down her hood.

Vaidehi.

Expression unreadable. Eyes locked on the door. Her hand moved into her coat pocket and pulled out a sleek chip—smooth, chrome, no markings. She didn’t rush. She attached it to the door handle like a routine she’d perfected.

Click.

The lock gave way with a polite blink of green.

She entered.

The flat was dimly lit from the hallway’s spill. There was a stillness inside, save for the faint hum of a fan and the soft snore of someone sleeping. Her eyes landed on the figure on the couch — Abhay, shirtless, arms sprawled, fast asleep.

She stepped closer, pulled a small mist canister from her pocket, and sprayed once — quiet, clinical.

He shifted slightly, but didn't wake.

Vaidehi leaned in just enough.

VAIDEHI

“Good night, toy,” she whispered, voice low and tinged with something cold.

Then she turned toward the hallway.

Bathroom door — closed.

She walked to it, hand reaching out for the knob.

Stopped.

A glowing red dot blinked at her.

Digital lock.

She blinked once, then let out a breath.

“Oh, shit.”

Vaidehi crouched down, unzipping the sleek black bag slung across her shoulder. Out came a compact laptop—matte finish, no branding—and the same unmarked chip she’d used to unlock the front door.

She connected the chip to the laptop with a short wire, then attached it firmly to the digital panel on the bathroom handle. Her fingers flew across the keyboard—controlled, rhythmic, surgical. No unnecessary flourish.

Lines of code danced on the screen.

She leaned in, muttering more to herself than to anyone else,

VAIDEHI

“Three-layer encryption on a bathroom door? What the hell were you hiding, Kritika?”

The lock clicked.

She didn’t open it right away.

Instead, she shut the laptop, coiled the wire with quiet precision, and placed everything neatly back into the bag. Then, with the silence of a shadow, she reached into her coat sleeve and slid out a small but sharp combat knife—black grip, curved edge.

She stood.

One breath.

The door creaked open just enough.

Steam billowed out as Vaidehi stepped in, her presence swallowed instantly by the haze.

Kritika stood with her back to the door, under the spray. Her long wet hair clung to her spine. She didn’t hear a thing.

Vaidehi moved behind her, fast—precise.

One silent inhale.

Then she drove the blade in—right below the left shoulder blade, angled with purpose.

Kritika jerked—sharp and sudden—but couldn’t scream. Her breath caught. Her limbs trembled for the briefest second.

Vaidehi pulled the knife back out cleanly.

Kritika's body gave out, but not forward. She collapsed backward, her soaked form slipping against the wet tiles, knees folding under her as she hit the floor hard.

Now she lay there—nude, limp, silent.

Eyes open.

Face turned upward, staring at the bathroom ceiling, unmoving.

Water from the shower rained down beside her, mixing with the slow bloom of red that pooled from beneath.

Vaidehi said nothing.

She just stood over the body for a second.

And blinked.

She zipped the bloodied knife into a plastic carry bag, then folded it neatly into a cloth pouch she’d stitched just for this.

From the very beginning, she’d been wearing gloves—thin, skin-colored, and tight against her fingers. No prints. No sweat. No slip.

She turned off the shower with a gloved hand, left the bathroom door slightly ajar, and stepped into Kritika’s bedroom.

Her eyes immediately locked onto the open laptop on the desk—screen still glowing, faint hum in the room.

She walked over, plugged her phone in with a wire, and launched her file bridge app.

A few quick keystrokes.

Ctrl + Alt + T — terminal open.

She ran a batch finder:

$ find ~/ -name vaidehi*
Dozens of hits popped up.

She skimmed.

One folder stood out:

DarkContract_ClientLogs

Inside, she found screenshots, transaction logs, PGP chat history, even an image of a wallet transfer.

Kritika had traced the dark web hitman contract—and it led directly to Vaidehi’s name and ID string.

Her breath hitched. “Shit…”

She tried to delete it.

$ rm -rf ~/DarkContract_ClientLogs

Access Denied.

She tried to kill its background process.

$ killall secguard

Still running.

She launched Task Manager, tried force-ending the service.

It restarted instantly.

She gritted her teeth. Opened the Registry Editor.

No access.

“Come on, come on…” she muttered, hands flying across keys.

Even tried masking it with a dummy folder:

$ mv DarkContract_ClientLogs ../old_cache/.null

The original came back—automatically restored.

But then—

Just as she leaned back in frustration—

DarkContract_ClientLogs

Deleting...
Deleting...
Removed Successfully.

The folder vanished.

Not into the bin. Not hidden.

Completely gone.

No trail.

No log.

She froze.

“…What the hell?” she whispered. “I didn’t even—”

Her jaw clenched.

“Who deleted it?”

She stared at the screen for half a second longer—

Then shut the laptop and yanked her phone out.

“Not now,” she muttered. “Not the damn time.”

She stuffed everything into her bag, did a fast room scan, then slipped her hoodie on.

Time wasted: at least eight minutes more than planned.

She opened the main door, paused, then walked out fast—hood up, gloves still on.

Vaidehi entered her room in the woods, closing the door quietly behind her. She immediately changed into comfortable clothes, shrugging off the tension, and sank into the chair by the small desk. A long sigh escaped her lips, carrying the weight of memories she couldn’t shake.

Her hand reached for a framed photo of Ishani on the desk. The moment her fingers touched it, her eyes glistened with unshed tears. For a while, she just stared, lost in the past.

—Jump to past—

Vaidehi stood in the gift maker’s small, cozy shop, holding a photograph carefully in her hands. The gift maker looked up, curiosity in her eyes.

“Mam… Miss Ishani must be really special for you,” she said, gesturing to the photo. “This looks… really great.”

Vaidehi’s lips curved into a soft, reminiscent smile. “Yes,” she replied quietly. “She is more than my sister. She is my everything. Whatever I am today, it’s only because of her. And this photo… this is for her. I want to gift it to her.”

After buying the photo frame, Vaidehi returned to the hotel where she and Ishani had been staying. As she approached, she noticed a crowd gathered near the entrance, murmuring anxiously. Police officers were questioning witnesses, their voices carrying over the commotion.

The hotel manager spotted her and pointed toward her. Moments later, a police officer approached, his expression serious.

“What was your relationship with Ishani?” he asked.

Vaidehi’s voice trembled, but she answered firmly, “She was my foster sister… she meant the world to me.”

The officer’s words hit like a blade. “She… she jumped into the ravine.”

Vaidehi froze, her world collapsing in an instant. She bent to the land, her body wracked with uncontrollable sobs. The officer reached out, trying to calm her, but nothing could reach her. Everything she knew, everything she had, seemed to vanish in that moment.

After the formalities, Vaidehi finally returned to the hotel room, still sobbing. As tears streamed down her face, her eyes fell on a letter lying on the bed.

Vaidehi picked up the letter and began to read, her hands trembling slightly. The familiar handwriting of Ishani filled the page:

"Vaidehi, I’m sorry I ditched you, but I couldn’t see any option. The company I was working for is threatening to kill me because I refused to participate in their shady dealings. I’m sorry I let you down, but believe me, this was the only choice I had. The reason for my suicide lies with the five involved in these deals. Destroy this letter and keep it out of reach of the police—I don’t want them to come after you. They are strong and powerful. I’m sorry."

Vaidehi folded the letter tightly, pressing it to her chest. A shiver ran through her, but her resolve hardened.

—Jump to Present—

“I’m sorry, Ishani… but I will avenge you,” she whispered, her voice low and determined. “I will destroy them all… four down, one left—and he will go down for sure.”

Her eyes glimmered with a mix of grief and fury, the fire of revenge igniting within her.
