Chapter 2

Cracks Begin to Show

Next day,

The cabin air was heavy. Manav, Satyam, and Kritika sat around a glass table, tension thick like smog.

MANAV (impatient):

"So? What’s the plan now? Keep smiling till the cops drag us out?"

KRITIKA (cool, flipping her hair):

"You’re underestimating Veer Khandelwal. He’s not some mall security guard. Man’s solved cases no one even understood."

MANAV (gritting teeth):

"And you think buttering him will save you?"

SATYAM (nervously adjusting his sleeves):

"Arguing won’t help. We stick to one line—'Lawyer present, no comments.' Simple."

Before anyone could answer, the cabin door slammed open.

Paras stormed in, grinning like he owned the place.

PARAS (loudly):

"Oye oye oye! No threesome without me, okay? I’ll be Kritika’s first! Pure love style, not the other dirty style you’re thinking!"

(Manav buried his face in his palms. Kritika shot daggers at Paras. Satyam looked like he regretted being born.)

KRITIKA (coldly):

"Paras, even mosquitoes have higher standards than you."

PARAS (shrugging, winking at Kritika):

"They also suck blood for free. I, at least, charge love and affection."

MANAV (gritting teeth harder):

"Get out, Paras. Serious discussion happening."

PARAS (plopping down on a chair, stretching his legs on the table):

"That’s why I came. Nothing screams 'panic' like a secret meeting. What’s next? Blood oath? Human sacrifice?"

SATYAM (muttering):

"Can we stick to the point?"

MANAV (leaning forward, voice low):

"We do not cooperate. No matter what they ask. No statements without lawyers. No emotional outbursts."

PARAS (grinning wider):

"Sounds boring. But fine. I’ll pretend to be innocent. Like always."

Kritika glanced at Manav, eyes sharp but calm.

Satyam kept fiddling with his pen, hands sweaty.

Paras leaned back, casually balancing the chair on two legs.

The room wasn’t a team. It was five grenades waiting for someone to pull the pin.

After a while,

The elevator doors parted with a faint ding, and Veer stepped into the chaos of Manav’s PR agency, Mehar trailing behind him, already sizing up the room with professional boredom.

The office was a mess of glass walls, neon signboards, and fake laughter.

Screens flashed campaign slogans, interns buzzed about like caffeinated mosquitoes, and someone was having a too-loud argument over influencer rates in the far corner.

Typical PR house, Veer thought — all gloss on top, all dirt underneath.

His eyes immediately found Kritika sitting lazily at a workstation near the glass wall.

Dressed in a fitted black shirt tucked neatly into grey cigarette pants, she looked like she belonged on the cover of a magazine, not stuck in this fluorescent-lit jungle.

Her loosely curled hair fell perfectly over one shoulder, her high heels tapped an absent rhythm on the floor, and the lazy confidence in her posture suggested she knew exactly the kind of effect she had on any room she walked into.

Paras, on the other hand, was stretched out across the couch like he owned the place — or maybe like he'd just lost consciousness there.

He wore a dark olive hoodie over beige chinos, sneakers kicked halfway off, and yet, with his sharp jawline and ruffled movie-star hair, he somehow managed to look like a misplaced Bollywood auditionee.

It was only when he opened his mouth and said things that people realized: God had gifted him a face, not a brain.

Satyam sat a few feet away at the meeting table, looking like he had an invisible stick permanently lodged somewhere unpleasant.

White shirt, navy trousers, spotless leather shoes — not a wrinkle out of place.

His glasses caught the light as he scribbled something on a legal pad, the stern lawyer aura practically forming a force field around him.

The type of guy who’d send you a defamation notice because you coughed too loudly near him.

Near the coffee machine, Arnav was battling technology with the desperate energy of a man trying to defuse a bomb.

Light blue checked shirt, black trousers, sneakers too clean for someone who actually worked — he was the type you forgot the minute you looked away.

His boyish face scrunched in frustration, unaware that the coffee machine was winning.

Then, from the far side, the door to a cabin swung open and out walked Manav — blazer sharp, hair sharper, and a smile that felt bought rather than born.

The deep maroon tie screamed expensive taste, and the way he casually adjusted his cufflinks suggested he knew exactly how good he looked.

The kind of man who didn't need to say he was in charge — the price tag on his watch did that for him.

Veer’s lips quirked into a small, sardonic smile.

Rich, pretty, and probably guilty as hell.

His favourite combination.

"Look what we have here," Veer muttered under his breath to Mehar. "Welcome to the jungle."

Manav started walking toward Paras, clearly about to say something in a low tone.

But Veer cut across the floor lazily and blocked his path with a smile that had the warmth of a frozen knife.

"Good morning, Manav," Veer said, voice light but eyes sharp.

"Mind if we have a little chat first? Before you start rehearsing your stories?"

Manav blinked, caught mid-step, then forced a neutral smile and nodded stiffly.

Veer didn't move aside.

He just stood there, letting the silence thicken like soup.

Mehar stayed near the entrance, pretending to check notes, but her watchful eyes missed nothing.

The suspects shifted slightly, like chess pieces realizing too late that the game had already started.

Manav cleared his throat, plastering a professional smile.

"Inspector, if you need anything, you can tell my assistant. I have a few urgent—"

"Perfect," Veer cut in smoothly, "then you won’t mind showing me the CCTV footage of your farmhouse from the night of Vaidehi’s death."

The air tightened.

For a beat, nobody moved.

Manav’s smile faltered. "Uh, the cameras... weren’t working that night. Power issues."

Veer raised an eyebrow, unimpressed.

"Power issues," he repeated flatly. "Must be contagious. Even your electricity knew something shady was about to happen?"

Before Manav could conjure another excuse, Paras piped up from his desk, his voice annoyingly loud.

"Power issues my ass. He smashed the cameras himself after the fight!"

A visible vein twitched at Manav’s temple.

"Paras," he said, the warning in his voice sharp enough to skin an animal.

Paras shrugged innocently, spinning lazily in his chair.

"What? Just telling the truth. Not like you can delete real life, bro."

Veer leaned back slightly, observing the tension with the amusement of a cat watching trapped mice.

He turned to Manav, voice casual.

"Well, now I'm really curious. Breaking your own cameras? Not suspicious at all. Makes my job super boring."

Manav straightened, trying to regain control.

"That’s not true. Paras is just—"

"An idiot? A compulsive blabbermouth? Sure," Veer interrupted with a lazy smirk. "But funny how idiots sometimes accidentally say the truth."

A thick silence settled again.

Mehar, standing by the door, jotted something down in her notepad, the scratch of her pen sounding way too loud in the heavy air.

Veer’s gaze swept across the room.

"And Vaidehi," he said casually, "she was working here, right?"

A hesitant nod from Kritika, who shifted uncomfortably under his stare.

"Office manager, right?" Veer continued.

"First Ishita, now Vaidehi. Damn, you guys really know how to retain employees... in hell."

Kritika flinched. Satyam cleared his throat. Paras tried and failed to hide a grin.

Manav’s knuckles whitened around the file he was holding.

Veer smiled — not kindly — and tapped the file in Manav’s hand.

"Word of advice," he said, his tone suddenly dropping cold, "lying to cops doesn’t save you. It just makes your funeral slightly more embarrassing."

He turned away, giving them one last lazy glance.

"Hope your acting skills are better than your security systems."

Without waiting for a response, Veer walked out.

Mehar followed, hiding her grin behind her notebook.

Behind them, the office seemed to exhale — but no one dared move yet.

The door clicked shut behind Veer and Mehar, leaving the office in a thick, uneasy silence.

For a few moments, no one moved.

The weight of Ishita’s name lingered in the air like something rotten.

Satyam finally broke the quiet, voice low and grim.

"First Vaidehi... now Veer’s poking around the Ishita case too. We can't sit quiet anymore."

Arnav leaned back in his chair, arms folded.

"We don’t have to sit quiet. We can start getting... proactive."

He cracked his knuckles meaningfully.

"Little muscle never hurt anyone."

Kritika shot him a sharp look.

"Save it, Rocky. You're not breaking anyone’s nose."

Satyam muttered,

"Yet."

Manav spoke with forced calm.

"We have nothing to fear. No proof, no dirt sticking to us. They can't find what doesn't exist."

The others exchanged uneasy glances, but no one contradicted him.

Manav continued, voice clipped.

"Still. Kritika — dig up whatever you can on this Veer. Friends, enemies, scandals, anything. Information is power."

Kritika gave a tight nod, already pulling her phone from her pocket.

Just then, the glass door swung open again, and a tall guy with a lean, athletic build and a wild mop of hair stumbled inside.

Paras grinned and threw his hands in the air.

"Look who’s here! Kritika’s unpaid intern."

The newcomer — Abhay — flashed a nervous smile, adjusting his too-loose backpack.

He wore a simple white T-shirt stretched just enough over his toned body, and faded jeans that had seen better days.

Paras gave an exaggerated once-over and whistled.

"Damn, Abhay. Look at that body! Size pack, tight chest — forget Kritika, come with me instead. I’ll pay better!"

Abhay blinked, caught like a deer in headlights.

"Uhh... I mean... Kritika will kill me if I say yes..."

Before Abhay could panic further, Arnav spoke up dryly from across the room:

"He's joking, you idiot."

Paras clutched his heart theatrically.

"Joking? No way! Arnav, be honest — you wouldn’t say no to this hunk, right? Come on, don’t be shy."

Arnav deadpanned,

"I mean... I can try— wait, no! I'm straight, you pervert!"

The room groaned collectively.

Kritika snapped her laptop shut and stood up, shooting a deadly glare around the room.

"Alright, clowns. Abhay, we’re leaving."

Abhay scratched his head.

"Leaving? But... Kritika, I really gotta hit the gym. I have a race coming up—"

"Gadget shopping," Kritika cut him off briskly.

"More important."

Abhay opened his mouth to argue, then thought better of it.

He followed Kritika out like a scolded puppy, while Paras called after them:

"Don't forget to buy him a leash!"

Kritika flipped him off over her shoulder without breaking stride.

The door swung shut behind them, leaving the rest of the team stewing in tension... and stupidity.

The sky outside had turned a lazy shade of orange, and inside the police station, Veer Khandelwal was just as lazy, half-asleep on his chair, one hand loosely dangling off the armrest.

Mehar entered quietly, arms crossed, watching him with an amused smile.

He actually looks kinda cute when he's not being a sarcastic jerk.

She tiptoed closer, grinning.

Then, at full volume, she shouted,

"I'M BORED!"

Veer jerked awake with a violent start, his chair nearly tipping over. He blinked at her, bleary-eyed.

"Good morning, sleepyhead," Mehar said sweetly, barely hiding her laughter.

"I was not sleeping," Veer grumbled, trying to smooth his hair, "I was... thinking."

"Sure you were," Mehar said, pulling a chair across from him.

"Why did you interrupt my deep... philosophical thinking?"

Mehar tossed a file onto his desk with a soft thud.

"Because while you were 'thinking', I was actually working. And guess what? I found something. Ishita and Vaidehi—they were sisters."

"Sisters?"

Mehar nodded.

"Yeah. Same mother, same father, just about four years apart. Ishita is the elder one."

Veer leaned back, tapping his pen against the desk.

"Family drama... always makes a case juicier."

"There's more," Mehar said.

"Ishita's case? It was ruled a suicide. Jumped off a cliff in Manali. But... they never found her body."

Veer whistled low.

"Lost in the beauty of Manali forever, huh?"

He shook his head.

"Still... no body? That's weird even for hill station suicides."

"Exactly," Mehar said, leaning forward.

"And the investigation back then? Half-assed. No autopsy, no deep inquiry, closed within two weeks."

Veer rubbed his face tiredly.

"Two dead girls. Same company. Same messy loose ends. I'm starting to see a pattern here... and it's uglier than Paras' sense of humour."

Mehar smirked.

"Should we reopen Ishita’s case officially?"

"Not yet," Veer said thoughtfully.

"If we poke too soon, they'll know we're onto them. Let them think we’re slow and lazy. Like me, five minutes ago."

Veer grunted and flipped the file open. His lazy expression tightened.

It was the autopsy report for Vaidehi Deshmukh.

Burnt beyond recognition.

Tissue damage too extensive.

No definitive cause of death could be confirmed.

The only match was through dental records and personal belongings.

"Useful," Veer said dryly, snapping the file shut.

"We have a body, but no answers."

Mehar sank into the chair opposite him.

"And worse... if Vaidehi was killed before being burned, we might never know how."

Veer scratched his jaw thoughtfully.

"Or if she even died at the farmhouse."

For a moment, both were silent, the dead-end hanging between them.

Then Mehar's phone buzzed sharply against the desk.

She glanced at the screen, frowned, picked it up.

"Mehar here... what? When? Shit, okay, we're coming."

Veer watched her hang up.

"Bad news?"

Mehar looked grim.

"Manav's employees really don't seem to have much job security."

Veer raised an eyebrow.

"Arnav — that gym rat? Found dead."

Veer stood up so fast his chair screeched back.

"When?"

"Half an hour ago," Mehar said, already grabbing her jacket.

"Looks fresh."

Veer grabbed his keys.

"Looks like the funeral train just added another compartment.

Let's move before they close the tracks."

They rushed out into the night, sirens ready to wail once more.
