Chapter 1

No Body, No Peace

The station was unusually quiet, which in Veer’s world meant two things: either criminals were on vacation, or karma was reloading.

Veer Khandelwal — tall, slightly disheveled, early 30s — sat with his legs stretched out over the dusty desk like he was auditioning for a sleep study. Sharp eyes, perfect stubble, and a general vibe of “I could solve this case, but I’d rather nap.”

A half-empty paper cup of chai was balanced on his knee. The fan above made more noise than it provided relief.

Veer thought to himself:

(A whole week of peace. Maybe my luck finally got bored of torturing me.)

A door creaked open, and Mehar Singh entered — sweet-faced but sharp-tongued, early 30s, with that deceptive charm people fall for just before realizing she’s emotionally bulletproof. Her hair was tied back in a messy bun today, and her tone was pure Monday morning homicide.

Mehar (briskly):

Veer. Up. Now. Murder at a farmhouse on the outskirts.

Veer didn’t flinch.

Veer (smirking):

Murder, huh? Take Hardik’s kid. He watches CID and likes dead bodies. I’m retired.

Mehar (with a sarcastic smile):

I would. But there’s no body. So technically, your favorite kind of case — the one where nobody can cry loudly around you.

Veer groaned, flopped his legs off the table, and squinted at her.

Veer thought to himself:

(No body? That’s suspicious. Or lazy. Either way, annoying.)

VEER (grumbling):

Alright. But if this turns out to be another drunk dude passed out in a bush, I’m sending you the dry-cleaning bill.

Mehar winked as she tossed him the keys.

Mehar (teasing):

And if it turns out to be something juicy, you owe me dinner. Shirtless.

Veer (rolling his eyes):

Mehar, stop flirting with me here.

She grinned and walked ahead. Veer stood up, grabbed his coat, and muttered as he followed her out.

Veer thought to himself:

(No body, no rest. Should’ve been a librarian.)

Midnight. A modern farmhouse stands on the outskirts of the city, stylish and cold. Spotlights from police cars cast long shadows over the driveway. The faint smell of smoke carries from the jungle nearby. A junior cop approaches as Veer and Mehar step out of their vehicle.

Junior Cop (pointing down the hill):

Sir, we just found the body about five minutes ago. Burned. Deep in the jungle. There's a blood trail from the bathroom upstairs leading all the way there.

Veer (eyeing Mehar):

You said there was no body.

Mehar:

There wasn’t. Five minutes ago.

Veer (sighing):

Great. Now I’m turned off.

Junior Cop (confused):

Sir?

Veer:

No one gets it. Never mind. Keep staring dramatically at the trees, you’re doing great.

They enter the farmhouse. The rooftop still reeks of last night’s party—spilled drinks, broken glasses, a speaker playing static. Everything is high-end and hollow.Veer stops at the entrance, scanning them.

Veer (mock whisper):

Okay… who looks the most like a killer here?

He spots Manav, slick hair, perfect shirt, phone still in hand like he’s waiting to call his lawyer.

Veer (tilting head):

Wow. Look at that guy. The CEO wrote all over him. Or maybe "cult leader." What’s his deal?

Mehar (rolling eyes):

Manav Rajput. Owner of Sygnex Dynamics. Manipulative, too clean, always escapes. We suspect he’s involved in three separate hush-up cases, but nothing stuck. Rumor says he forces interns to read his autobiography.

Veer:

Haan, looks like the kind of guy who'd charge rent to stand next to him.

Next, Veer notices Kritika, leaning back with crossed arms, confident, hoodie on, chewing gum.

Veer (low whistle):

Well well, who's the hot girl with a hacker attitude?

Mehar (sharp):

The “hot girl” is Kritika Narang. Cybersecurity head. Hacked her university once, then got hired to stop people like her. Also possibly hacked her ex’s crypto wallet.

Veer (smirking):

Hacker and heartbreaker. Dangerous combo.

Mehar (mutters):

You want her number, too?

Veer (mock serious):

Please. I’ve got enough women plotting my murder already.

He turns to Satyam, awkward posture, oversized suit, looks like a lost intern. His glasses fog up when he breathes.

Veer:

Wait. Who’s that nerd?

Mehar (checking notes):

Satyam Bansal. Legal head. Doesn’t look like it, but he’s protected three cases from reaching court. Wears guilt like cologne. Probably hasn’t slept since 2019.

Veer:

Bro, looks like he sues people for not returning pens.

Next is Paras, confident, grinning at Mehar already, shirt half-buttoned on purpose.

Veer (smirking):

Ah. And this one thinks he’s the main character.

Mehar:

Paras Dhingra. PR head. Too charming for his own good. Got out of three harassment complaints using the words “mutual misunderstanding.”

Veer:

PR? Makes sense. Smiles like he blackmails exes in HD.

Last is Arnav, muscular, resting bouncer face, arms folded. Silent but intense.

Veer:

And here we have the one who actually could have carried a body through the jungle.

Mehar:

Arnav Bhatt. Field ops guy. Security, logistics. Ex-bouncer. Almost beat up a cop once during a raid. That cop was me.

Veer (grinning):

You flirting with him?

Mehar:

No. I still have the medical report.

Veer (steps back, scanning all of them):

Alright. Five suspects. One dead girl. One real burned body. And a cop who was just trying to nap today. Let’s make this quick before someone else gets murdered — ideally not me.

(He claps once and signals to the team to split up. Suspects look around nervously.)

Veer (to himself):

(This better not turn into one of those “everyone's guilty” things.)

The farmhouse living room is dimly lit, chairs scattered like bad decisions. The suspects sit uneasily. Veer strolls in, coffee in hand, like he owns the crime scene.

He glances at Mehar.

Veer:

"Let’s meet the charming people who survived the party."

He walks up to Maanav, sitting with his arms crossed, calm like a man used to dodging accountability.

Veer:

"You look like the kind of guy who sues people for blinking too loud."

Manav:

"I’m not saying anything without my lawyer."

Veer (sips coffee):

"Great. Use Satyam. He’s right here."

Manav:

"I said lawyer, not a failed YouTube ad."

Satyam (offended, from the side):

"I passed the bar!"

Veer (dryly):

"Which one? The one with happy hours?"

He turns to Kritika, who sits like she’s bored with everything, especially the law.

Veer:

"Wow. Beauty and the beast in one party. And you’re both."

Mehar (immediately):

“She’s already got three cyberbullying complaints. Two victims ended up in therapy.”

Kritika (coldly):

"Add me to your trauma collection. I didn’t kill anyone."

Paras (grinning from the back):

"Hey Kritika, my baby, is this guy bothering you? Want me to call the cops?”

Kritika (without looking at him):

"Paras, shut up before I kill you."

Paras (mock gasp):

"She’s joking about murder. That’s evidence, right?"

Veer raises an eyebrow, walks toward Paras with mild regret on his face.

Veer:

"You. I don’t even know what to say to you anymore."

Paras:

"That’s what my therapist said, too."

Veer:

"Where were you during the murder?"

Paras:

"Following my dream. It ended up in the fridge."

Mehar (without looking up):

“He's a social media manager. Got fired from three agencies before this. One was for livestreaming a breakup.”

Paras (to Kritika, winking):

"Hey, if this interrogation stresses you out, just imagine Veer in beach shorts."

Veer:

"Get out."

Veer walks to Satyam, who looks like a lawyer even when he’s not trying—thin, anxious, and holding a file no one asked for.

Veer:

"You look like you’d press charges on a vending machine."

Satyam:

"I’m not answering any questions."

Veer:

"You just did."

He finally approaches Arnav, who’s built like a fridge and has the emotional range of one.

Veer:

"Let me guess. You think punching your problems is conflict resolution?"

Arnav (coolly):

"I didn’t touch anyone."

Paras (loudly, faking fear):

"You beat me just like last time! Cop, save me, I’m the VICTIM HEREEEEEE."

Arnav (rolls his eyes, waves a fist):

"Keep talking and I’ll make it a habit."

Veer (chuckles):

"You five are gonna make this week interesting."

Mehar:

"You say that like you’re not already dying inside."

They walk out, leaving the suspects inside. Outside near the parking area, they stand under a flickering light. It’s quiet for a second. Then Veer sighs.

Veer:

"Not a single one of them looks innocent."

Mehar:

"That’s because they aren’t. Welcome to the company party."

After a while, Veer stood in the dim farmhouse bathroom, hands in his pockets, eyeing the dark, sticky puddle on the floor.

Veer:

(squinting at the blood)

“Classic head trauma. Romantic.”

Blood smeared outwards in long streaks—drag marks—from the bathroom to the back door. He followed them out silently, the moonlight catching on the muddy trail winding into the forest behind the house.

A junior cop stood stiff near the scorched patch in the woods, trying not to breathe too deeply.

Junior Cop:

“Sir… body was found here. Burned. Pretty bad.”

Veer crouched down near the blackened remains, his voice deadpan.

Veer:

“Ah. Nothing like the smell of overcooked mystery to ruin a night off.”

Mehar:

(walking up beside him, pinching her nose)

“Yeah, she smells like my first attempt at cooking chicken. Black outside, raw story inside.”

The junior cop blinked, unsure whether to laugh or cry.

Veer noticed the half-burned handbag, IDs slightly melted but readable. He poked at it with his pen.

Veer:

“Hit in the bathroom. Dragged. Burned. And ID cards are perfectly fine? The killer's either dumb or dramatic.”

He stood up, brushing his hands like the scene had physically dirtied him.

Veer:

(to the junior cop)

“Lift prints from the bag. DNA tests the ashes. Check her call records—anyone who texted her ‘You up?’ more than twice this week, bring them in.”

The cop scurried off.

Veer turned to Mehar, not even looking at her, just tossing the line over his shoulder.

Veer:

“Get me a background check. Family. Friends. Exes. Anyone she blocked in the last month?”

Mehar:

(casually)

“Sure. But only if you finally take me out after this.”

Veer:

(pauses)

“Bold request during a burnt corpse analysis.”

Mehar:

(grinning)

“Well, you know I’m into guys who smell like evidence.”

Veer rolled his eyes, a smile threatening the corner of his mouth as he stepped back, watching the ashes scatter slightly in the wind.

Veer:

“This isn’t just murder. It’s art. Ugly, loud, and trying too hard to be noticed.”
