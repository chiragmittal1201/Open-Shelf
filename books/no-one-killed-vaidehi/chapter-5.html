Chapter 5

When the Screens Went Silent

A crowd has gathered around the twisted remains of a car wreck. Barricade tape flutters in the morning breeze. The air smells of burning rubber and scraped metal. Satyam’s car is unrecognizable — the front half crushed into the rear end of a parked truck.

Police officers are scattered, trying to control the curious onlookers. Kritika stands on the edge, arms folded, staring at the wreck with unreadable eyes.

Veer and Mehar are already there, circling the scene. Mehar holds a voice note playing on her phone.

MEHAR

(gesturing to the wreck, phone playing softly)

This is from the mechanic. Brake repair was included.

But the expert just checked — says the brakes weren’t working since the car left the garage.

It’s technically possible they failed after, during the day… but that’s rare.

VEER

(eyes on the wreckage)

Hmm.

Brakes don’t usually fail that quickly.

And they sure don’t look tampered with.

(sighs)

Okay, do one thing. Call the car mechanic.

Bring him to the ghost room — the one where we took Raghu.

Let’s see if we can rattle some screws loose.

MEHAR

(nods)

On it.

Also — truck was parked here overnight. The driver had gone home to sleep.

He’s here though.

VEER

(squints at the TRUCK DRIVER nearby)

You!

Truck’s getting seized. You’ll pay a fine — and congratulations — six months in lockup.

That’s not a parking spot, champ.

TRUCK DRIVER

(terrified)

Sir! Please! Jail for wrong parking?

MEHAR

(grinning)

Come on, VIP room’s not that bad.

Don't cry. You might get a fan in there.

(she takes him away)

Veer turns and spots Kritika, still staring at the mangled vehicle.

He walks over, casual as ever.

VEER

(smirks)

Well well… hacker in daylight? That’s rare.

What happened? Did the internet shut down?

KRITIKA

(sternly)

What happened to Satyam?

VEER

Brake failure.

Crashed hard. Died on the spot.

But here’s the twist — the car was repaired day before yesterday.

KRITIKA

(cuts in)

No — he picked the car up yesterday.

He told me. Morning he got dropped to office… picked it up in the evening.

VEER

(invoice still in hand)

But the invoice says the day before yesterday.

KRITIKA

Maybe the garage backdated it? I’m telling you what he said.

VEER

(thinking)

Hmm.

Someone’s killing you people. One by one.

And I still don’t know why.

Kid, Tell me, what exactly did your happy little company do?

You all pissed someone off in a very permanent way.

KRITIKA

(annoyed)

Don’t call me kid.

You can call me Kritika. And it’s your job to protect us — which clearly isn’t going great.

Anyway — did the brakes look tampered with?

VEER

(stares at her for a beat)

I won’t say.

KRITIKA

Why not?

VEER

Because you could do something…

But your lawyer’s dead. So no.

(turns to leave)

If I were you… I’d start worrying about your team.

Veer walks away, leaving Kritika alone beside the wreck, the sound of sirens fading in the background.

After a while,

Paras walks into the station lobby with the swagger of a man who thinks he’s the main character. He looks around like he owns the place, mumbling to himself.

PARAS

(looking around)

Hmm… where’s the butter to my paratha?

(eyes lock on Mehar behind a desk)

There she is.

He casually strolls over to Mehar, grinning like a schoolboy with a stolen lunchbox.

PARAS

Hello, hello, hello... ACP Mehar.

Did you forget to invite me for lunch or were you planning to arrest me for breaking hearts again?

MEHAR

(rolls her eyes)

Paras? What are you doing here?

Forgot Kritika’s address or did the GPS in your head break again?

PARAS

(grinning)

Aww... you sound jealous.

If I knew you missed me this much, I’d have shown up sooner.

I’m sorry you had to suffer... silently… all this time.

MEHAR

(straightens up, frowning)

Why are you like this?

Clumsy, clueless… and why do you always think there was ever something between us?

PARAS

(defensively, but with charm)

Woah woah woah—

I never said that.

But hey, you’re not exactly forgettable.

You’re… you’re beautiful.

MEHAR

(staring)

I wasn’t underestimating myself, thanks.

Wait—

Did you just say I’m beautiful?

PARAS

(suddenly in poetic mode, exaggerated)

Of course!

Your eyes? Like freshly brewed filter coffee — hot and dangerous.

Your walk? Like a storm warning with lipstick.

And that voice? If sass had a ringtone, it’d be you.

Even your shoulder holster somehow flirts with me.

Every curve, every sigh — God really had a field day sculpting you.

MEHAR

(amused and blushing slightly)

Okay, calm down Bollywood.

Then tell me this — if I’m so perfect, why does Veer never say yes to a single date?

Every time I ask, he ducks like I threw a grenade at him.

PARAS

(mock serious)

That’s because he’s a dumb cop.

A fool in uniform.

If I were Veer?

(chuckles)

I’d have promoted you to Head of Maharashtra Police if you’d just agreed to be my girlfriend.

VEER

(offscreen, dry as a desert)

Interesting hiring standards.

Both Paras and Mehar turn — Veer’s leaning against the doorway, hands in pocket, unreadable expression on his face.

PARAS

(grinning)

Hey, Officer Headmaster.

How’s the discipline patrol going?

VEER

(deadpan)

Mind telling me what you’re doing here… and why exactly you’re molesting my colleague?

MEHAR

(interrupting, rolling her eyes)

He wasn't molesting me, Veer.

VEER

(sternly, eyes locked on Paras)

He was talking about your curves.

Your body.

I will flag it as molestation.

And if he doesn’t want to die mysteriously in an encounter, he better stay away from you.

PARAS

(raising eyebrows)

I’m right here, Officer Angry-Pants.

You can say it to me directly, no need for a press release.

(pauses, smirking)

Besides, nobody can kill me… except maybe the writer.

(looks at the ceiling)

Right, boss?

MEHAR

(blinks)

What?

VEER

(staring blankly for a second, then snapping back)

What did you say?

I’ll ask one last time—what the hell are you doing here?

PARAS

(singsong)

To see you, my sweetu.

VEER

(snaps, furious)

You’re under arrest.

PARAS

(taking a step back, mock offended)

No thank you.

I don’t like being touched inappropriately… even by cops.

Besides, I got what I came for.

(turns, walking backwards)

Hey Veer, if you ever feel alone… you know who to call.

(blows a kiss)

VEER

(shouting)

GET. OUT. NOW.

[Paras leaves, still grinning. Mehar tries to hide her laugh. Veer fumes silently, jaw clenched.]

After a while,

Paras’s cluttered apartment. Neon lighting. He's sitting in front of his laptop, ring light glowing, messy shelf behind him. Half a packet of chips and two soda cans on the table. The live chat is buzzing.

Paras

(grinning)

“Heyyy my beautiful degenerates, guess who’s live again after, like… forever?”

He waves with both hands, gives a fake pageant smile, then slouches back casually.

Paras

“Yeah yeah, I know. I’m home. Shocking. Wait—”

He squints at the chat.

Paras

(reading aloud, mockingly)

“‘Bro, are you okay? Why are you at home, did you forget the bar's address?’”

He laughs.

Paras

“Honestly? Even my sofa just asked who the hell I am. Trust me, I hate being home. It smells like regret and unpaid bills.”

Another comment pops up, he reads.

Paras

“‘Do that bar livestream again, the one where you called the bartender ‘dad’ by mistake.’”

(grins)

“Oh you mean the legendary stream where I accidentally exposed my boss’s affair while drunk? Classic.”

He leans closer, dropping the act a bit.

Paras

“But tonight’s stream isn’t about alcohol, unfortunately. It’s about the truth. And maybe a little revenge.”

He smirks. Picks up a flash drive, dangles it in front of the camera.

Paras

“Let’s just say… if Officer Veer is watching — hi sweetie — tell Mehar I love her. I mean, if she ever stops rejecting people like it’s an Olympic sport.”

He chuckles, pauses.

Paras

“You know, if I were Veer, I’d have made Mehar the Head of Maharashtra Police by now. Just saying. One date, ma’am. That’s all I ask.”

He presses a button, starting to load a video. Just then, we subtly hear the faintest shift — a door creaks ever so slightly in the background… but Paras doesn’t notice.

Paras

(to the chat)

“Okay people, time for some drama. The kind that ruins careers and ends fake love stories. You’re welcome.”

The screen flickers just a bit — as if the Wi-Fi is being tampered with...

Paras

(annoyed)

“Wait, why is the upload—”

Cut to black.

Outside a plush high-rise apartment, Kritika storms toward the front door. A private security guard stands firm, blocking her path.

Guard

(gently, hesitant)

“Ma’am, sir’s given strict instructions—no one’s allowed inside right now. He’s… busy.”

Kritika

(sternly, not stopping)

“I know exactly who he’s busy with. Move.”

She shoves past. The guard steps aside, watching her go, and murmurs under his breath with a strangely calm tone—

Guard

(softly, with a hint of weight)

“…Wouldn’t stop you even if I could, ma’am. Some doors open on their own.”

Kritika doesn’t hear him. She bangs the apartment door open, walks in furiously.

Kritika

(voice raised, sharp)

“GET. OUT. NOW!”

Two girls — barely dressed — scream and scramble from the sofa, grabbing whatever pieces of clothing they can find in panic.

Manav

(still shirtless, leaning back on the couch like this is routine)

“God, Kritika. Why must you always show up during my quality time?”

He looks her up and down with a playful smirk.

Manav

“You’re jealous, aren’t you? Tell me—should I clear the schedule next time just for you? Replace these… chicks with your sass?”

Kritika folds her arms, expression blank but eyes blazing. The two women finally find enough clothing to cover up and, upon hearing Manav's shameless flirt, glare at him in disgust. One scoffs, the other mutters something under her breath. Both storm out past Kritika without a word.

Kritika stands in the center of the room, her eyes hard, as she stares at Manav. The atmosphere is heavy, no humor, no pretense.

Kritika

(seriously)

“Enough with the jokes. Arnav and Satyam... both of them are dead.”

Manav

(leans back, half-smiling, nonchalantly)

“Yeah, I know. It's hard to find replacements for guys like that. No one with connections like Arnav or a lawyer as good as Satyam.”

Kritika

(eyes sharp, voice tightening)

“We’re being targeted, Manav. Someone’s after us. Stop pretending you know everything. We need to do something now.”

Manav

(slightly smirking, nonchalant)

“Oh? Is that true? Then tell me — who's next? We’re all right here. Where’s Paras, though? I told him to dig up dirt on Veer.”

He pulls out his phone, starts scrolling through it with ease.

Manav

(as he looks at the screen, muttering to himself)

“I knew he’d find something. Yeah, he’s live streaming right now.”

Kritika

(dismissive, with a hint of irritation)

“Veer’s clean, Manav. He must have made it all up himself.”

Manav

(laughing under his breath, leaning back smugly)

“Who cares? I knew him before you did. You know, I’m not that dumb, Kritika.”

He pauses for a moment, his expression turning more serious as his eyes flick to the screen.

Manav

(thinking aloud)

“I’ve been keeping an eye on this. The killer—he only goes after the ones who are alone. And right now? Paras is live, and we’re all together.”

He shrugs as if the danger is a mere inconvenience.

Manav

(half-smiling)

“So, chill. We’re safe. That’s why I always surround myself with the best company.”

Kritika, still tense, feels a small relief, but the nagging sense of danger doesn’t quite leave. Manav, however, seems too relaxed.

Manav

(eyes narrowed, frustrated)

“Wait… why is the internet so slow? What the hell is going on, losers?”

A servant enters the room cautiously, sensing the change in tone.

Servant

(politely, but nervous)

“Sir, everything is working fine with the internet.”

Manav

(irritated, snapping)

“Then why is this livestream cut off?!”

Kritika pulls out her phone and checks, her eyes widening.

Kritika

(voice tight with urgency)

“The livestream's gone off, something’s wrong… Paras—he’s gone offline.”

She stands up quickly, anxiety rising in her chest.

Kritika

(urgently, almost shouting)

“Let’s go, now! We need to check on him.”

Without wasting another second, they both rush out the door, Manav throwing one last glance at his phone screen, his face darkening slightly.

The sound of rushed footsteps.

Manav and Kritika storm toward the front door. But two police officers are already at the scene, one of them being the familiar junior cop Veer had recently advised.

MANAV

(snapping)

What the hell are you doing here?

The junior cop raises an eyebrow — calm, unfazed.

JUNIOR COP

Question is, sir… what are you doing here?

And since when are we supposed to tell you anything?

MANAV

(gritting his teeth)

Because this is my house. The deed’s in my name.

Now tell me—what happened to Paras?

The junior cop doesn't answer immediately. Instead, he steps slightly aside, revealing Paras’s lifeless body sprawled inside the living room.

Manav stops. His body stiffens, eyes locked on his friend. The color drains from his face—not in fear, but rage barely contained.

MANAV

(quieter, guttural)

What the f*** happened?

He looks like he’s holding a scream inside his chest.

JUNIOR COP

(softer now)

Primarily facie… heart attack.

KRITIKA

(startled)

What?

The junior cop steps back and pulls out his phone. He dials, ignoring incoming calls.

JUNIOR COP

(on phone)

Sir, Paras is dead. Looks like he got a heart attack.

INT. POLICE STATION – SAME TIME

Veer is sipping tea, glancing at a noticeboard, tired eyes darting over scribbled notes and timelines. His phone buzzes.

VEER

(answering, cold)

I don't think so.

(pause)

I’m coming.

He ends the call and turns his head slightly.

VEER

(calling out, dry)

Mehar. Come here.

Mehar walks in, holding a folder.

VEER

Paras is dead.

MEHAR

(blinking, then slowly)

Wait. He's… actually dead?

VEER

(eyeing her)

You sound surprised.

MEHAR

(dry, a little defensive)

I just thought… if someone ever shot him, the bullet would change direction out of sheer annoyance.

VEER

(sighing)

Let’s go.

The room is dim. Paras’s body lies lifeless, half in shadow. Officers move around the scene carefully.

Veer and Mehar step inside, both scanning the room with instinctive precision.

MANAV

(sharp, bitter)

This is how you do your duty?

Let people die… then investigate… and close the case with a coffee and a quote?

You closed Ishani’s file too. Just like that.

At the mention of Ishani, something flickers in Veer’s eyes. A pause. He doesn’t reply, but the name clearly lands somewhere deeper than he lets on.

He shifts his focus back.

VEER

(calmly)

Why are you so… hyper for Paras, Manav?

MANAV

(finally losing it, voice cracking)

Because he was like my brother!

The outburst silences the murmuring officers. A moment of raw, real pain slips into the cold room.

Before it spirals, Kritika grabs Manav’s arm, pulling him a few steps away.

KRITIKA

(urgent, low)

Hey. You’re not helping.

Getting hyper here won’t bring him back… and it’ll only bring us trouble.

Manav breathes heavily, his rage slowly cooling.

MANAV

(regretful)

I shouldn’t have brought Ishani into this.

KRITIKA

(soft but firm)

You shouldn’t have. Not like this.

They stay back in a quiet corner, shadows of grief etched into both their faces.

In the house,

Veer crouches beside the body, his eyes moving from the body to the desk… to the unplugged modem.

JUNIOR COP

(reporting)

Paras’s position suggests no physical struggle.

The body’s cold, no signs of forced entry, and oddly enough… the internet cable was cut.

MEHAR

(frowning)

Cut? Like… clean-cut?

JUNIOR COP

Yes, clean. Snipped from the back of the router.

We're still trying to figure out why he’d cut it himself before dying.

VEER

(tilting his head)

You're assuming he did.

JUNIOR COP

Well, there's no sign of anyone else. No foreign fingerprints, no footprints, no disturbance…

VEER

(rising, murmuring)

Hmm.

He walks slowly around the room. His eyes scan for subtle shifts—dust, shadows, odd absences.

His hand grazes the curtain corner, then the edge of the table, not really touching, just… tracing.

A thought is forming.

VEER

(to himself)

No signs of struggle. Internet cut cleanly.

Livestream ends minutes before death.

Too neat.

He looks at the back door, the window lock, and the unopened snack packet on the counter.

Something isn't adding up—and his face says he knows it.

JUNIOR

Sir, strange thing is—Paras barely stayed home. Mostly crashed at his office or parties.

No house help either, from what I found.

MEHAR

(nods)

Yeah. He called it his “bachelor graveyard.” Never bothered cleaning. Used to brag about it too.

VEER

(looking around)

Then why does every surface look like it’s been scrubbed with a guilt complex?

(He crouches, eyes narrow)

And look at this—internet wire’s cut. Not chewed. Not torn. Clean slice. You don’t get that from a rat... unless the rat knows how to use scissors.

MEHAR

(quietly)

Could it be a hitman?

JUNIOR

(considering)

Possible. Paras was live. Suddenly goes offline. No signs of a struggle.

Cutting the net first makes sense if you didn’t want the kill streamed.

VEER

(calm but final)

Then it wasn’t a panic kill. Someone came in, killed him, wiped all traces, and left.

A hitman—or someone who knows how not to get caught.

MANAV

(tense, keeping his voice low)

We need to find out who hired him. Paras wasn’t important enough to be killed randomly.

KRITIKA

(nodding)

I’ll start from the hitman side. You try to find out who paid for the job.

They unlock the car. Kritika doesn’t realize her hands are shaking slightly.

Veer sits at Paras’s desk, eyes flicking over an open mail tab on the laptop. His finger hovers, then clicks on a Telegram message window. He reads silently:

KRITIKA (Text Message):

The warden isn’t talking about Ishani or Vaidehi.

Find some dirt on him. Enough to scare him into opening up.

Veer freezes. His brow furrows. Something clicks.

VEER

(muttering to himself)

This is what I was missing...

He closes the lid slowly and stands.

VEER

(calling out)

Mehar. Wrap it up. Junior—you too. Send the body to forensics.

He heads toward the door. Without looking back:

VEER

We’ll get a report saying he was drugged.

That was no heart attack. That was a damn message.

Mehar and Junior Cop Sharma enter, exhausted but alert.

MEHAR

(to constable)

Paras’s body is in for autopsy, right? Get me the status the second the report comes.

CONSTABLE

Yes, ma’am.

MEHAR

Where’s Veer sir?

CONSTABLE

He left just five minutes ago. Came in about twenty minutes back, looked through some old files, and left.

JUNIOR (SHARMA)

(squinting)

He’s onto something again. Definitely didn’t wait for us.

MEHAR

Then let’s not waste time either, Sharmaji.

They sit at a desk, flipping through the photographs taken from Paras’s apartment.

Just then, a constable walks over holding a small envelope.

CONSTABLE

Ma’am, this just arrived. From the tech team—package for Veer sir.

JUNIOR (SHARMA)

Ah, that must be the broken CCTV footage we were trying to recover from the farmhouse.

MEHAR

Let’s see if there’s anything useful in it.

She plugs in the pen drive. The screen flickers, static rolls in and out, and then a shaky video starts playing. A distant camera angle shows someone—vaguely female in figure—dragging something heavy through the forested area.

JUNIOR (SHARMA)

(enlarging the footage)

That’s... that’s someone dragging a body.

They keep watching. The girl—slender, long-haired—pulls out something wrapped in cloth, along with what looks like a jerry can and lighter fluid. She stashes them under a large bush, then walks out of the frame.

MEHAR

(squinting)

Footage timestamp is from early morning. Just hours before the murder night.

JUNIOR (SHARMA)

(quietly, staring at screen)

Same height… same build... It could be Vaidehi.
