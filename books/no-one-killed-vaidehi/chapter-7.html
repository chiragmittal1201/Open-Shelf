Chapter 7

The Bullet that Spoke for Her

Back at the station, Veer, Mehar, and Sharmaji huddled around the case files, the tension thick in the room.

SHARMAJI

The problem is… the video footage hints that Vaidehi might be alive. The body we found was brought in the morning, and we don’t even know what to do next.

MEHAR

Kritika’s body is also in suspense. Whoever killed her had access to her house keycards. And she was killed in the bathroom, naked. Abhay was sleeping hard, probably under the influence of sleeping powder. We woke him up, and from his expression and the medical report, we can conclude he was out cold.

Veer leaned back in his chair, rubbing his chin.

VEER

Hmm… I don’t know. I was just… looking at Kritika. I mean, the killer made it so we wouldn’t resist. I mean… what a hot Kritika was looking naked. Thanks, my job.

Mehar shot him a sharp glare.

MEHAR

Veer! Focus! What are we supposed to do?

VEER

Our security around Kritika and Manav failed. Kritika’s already dead. Manav is the next target for sure… though he doesn’t deserve it. But we need to give him high-end protection.

SHARMAJI

Should I bring Manav to our ghost room?

MEHAR

He won’t fall for it, Sharmaji. But… bring him anyway. We’ll make him talk somehow.

Manav sat in his cabin, the office weirdly quiet. Varsha, his assistant, entered with a tray.

VARSHA

Sir, your tea.

Manav waved her over, his eyes scanning the empty office.

MANAV

Varsha… why does this office look like hell emptied out?

Varsha hesitated.

VARSHA

Sir… they’re gone. You can’t just bring it up every time I come.

He let out a heavy sigh.

MANAV

He was my brother… Paras. I don’t know what to do without him. I thought… he was dumb enough not to know how to die, but… he’s gone. And Kritika… she’s gone too. Now… who will tell me what to do?

Varsha leaned closer, her tone soft and flirtatious.

VARSHA

Sir… I’m here. You know I can cheer you up right now… even here in the office.

Manav shook his head.

MANAV

No… now is not the time. And I… I got bored of you.

At that moment, the office doors burst open. Police officers, led by Sharmaji, entered. Before Manav could react, a constable stepped forward and handcuffed him.

MANAV

What is this for? We are the ones being targeted, and you’re arresting us? I have rights! Arrest the killer, not me!

SHARMAJI

I don’t care. I’m ordered to arrest you. And I will do it.

Back in the woods, Vaidehi switched on the TV. The news flashed across the screen:

Breaking: Manav, founder and CEO of the city’s biggest PR company, has been arrested. Reasons for the arrest are currently unknown…

Her eyes narrowed as she switched off the TV abruptly.

VAIDEHI

If he goes to jail… I won’t be able to kill him. It’s now or never.

Vaidehi quickly buckled up, slipped back into her hoodie, and opened a small locker in the almirah. She drew out a gun, holding it firmly in her hands.

VAIDEHI

Ishani… this is for you. I don’t know if I’ll return from this… but I can’t see any other way.

With anger and focus burning in her eyes, she stormed off into the night.

Manav was being escorted in the police jeep with Sharmaji and two constables. As they neared the car, the jeep screeched to a sudden halt. A figure in a hoodie, face hidden, stepped out and fired at the handcuffs, shattering them.

Manav instinctively scrambled out of the jeep. The hooded girl stood in front of him, gun leveled directly at him.

MANAV

Vaidehi? I didn’t think you’d pull a stunt like this.

From behind, Veer arrived just in time.

VEER

Vaidehi, stop! You can’t do this! We’ll arrest you for a lifetime sentence — stop!

But Vaidehi didn’t flinch. Her eyes were locked on Manav.

Veer stepped forward, hands raised.

VEER

Vaidehi—listen to me. Calm down. Ishani was your foster sister. She died, I know. But killing them won’t bring her back. This isn’t right. Put the gun down. We can talk. We’ll sort this out.

Vaidehi’s laugh was small and bitter.

VAIDEHI

Veer, shut up. You don’t know anything. It wasn’t suicide. He threatened her. He forced her. She didn’t jump—he pushed her.

Manav’s face went white, then hard.

MANAV

If she couldn’t handle pressure, what did I do wrong? We ran a business. We pushed people, sure. That’s how you survive. Employees quit all the time—

VAIDEHI

You know what I’m talking about. Ishani knew about your blackmail, Manav. She told you ‘no.’ You threatened her. You made her a target. You drove her to this.

Around them, officers murmured. Veer’s eyes flicked between Vaidehi and Manav, searching for the crack that would prove the truth from grief. But Vaidehi wasn’t looking for proof now—only justice.

Two months later, the station was quieter, the air less tense, though scars of the past case still lingered. Sharmaji walked in, his limp barely noticeable now. Mehar spotted him and smiled faintly.

MEHAR

Sharmaji, how are you feeling? Good after recovery?

Sharmaji nodded.

SHARMAJI

All good, ma’am. Honestly, I never thought I’d recover from the accident Vaidehi caused. Life gave me a second chance, I guess. But… tell me one thing. How did Veer sir manage to save Manav that night?

Mehar’s expression darkened.

MEHAR

Veer couldn’t save him. Vaidehi shot him before we could do anything. We did manage to catch her though. She didn’t run. She accepted responsibility for every single murder.

SHARMAJI

But why? Why go that far?

MEHAR

When Ishani found out Manav and his gang were blackmailing rich people—digging up dirt and threatening them—she refused to be a part of it. They cornered her, threatened to kill her if she didn’t join. Out of fear, out of pressure, she took her own life. And when Vaidehi learned the truth, she didn’t see the law as an option. She joined their company only to destroy them… one by one. It was revenge, Sharmaji. Nothing more, nothing less.

Sharmaji stood frozen, stunned by the weight of it all.

The silence that followed was heavier than any words he could muster.
