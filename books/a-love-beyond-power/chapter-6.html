Chapter 6

The Clash

Rhea stood in front of her mirror, taking a deep breath. The morning sunlight filtered through the windows, casting a warm glow over the room. It had been a long time since she had stood in front of this mirror with the same sense of confidence. Today, she felt different.

Her hair, once unkempt with stress, was now neatly styled in a soft wave, cascading over her shoulders. The faintest trace of makeup highlighted her sharp features—her eyes, usually clouded with doubt, now burned with clarity. Her skin had a healthy glow, as if shedding the weight of the past few months.

She wore a fitted cream blouse, tucked neatly into high-waist black trousers. The fabric clung to her frame, elegant and professional, but it was the way she wore it that made the outfit speak volumes: like she was in control again. The outfit was a subtle reflection of her transformation—her first step towards reclaiming her life.

She looked at herself in the mirror. The woman staring back at her was not the same girl who had fled from her responsibilities. This was a woman who had found her way back to her purpose.

Her lips parted as she spoke softly to herself, her voice steady, yet tinged with determination. “You’ve got this, Rhea. You know what you’re doing.”

She paused, her eyes narrowing, her gaze sharpening as she focused on the reflection. “This is for you, Dad. For the legacy you trusted me with. And for myself.”

The words felt heavy, but as she spoke them, they also felt true. She straightened her back, the weight she had carried over the past months lifting ever so slightly. With one final look at her reflection, she nodded to herself. This was her time to step up.

Rhea walked into the Kapoor Fashion headquarters, her heels clicking sharply against the polished marble floor. The building that once hummed with the energy of creativity and ambition now felt strangely still, like a shadow of its former self. She could almost feel the weight of the silence hanging in the air, as if the walls themselves were holding their breath, waiting for her return.

The atmosphere was tense. The sleek offices, which once felt like a hub of ideas and innovation, now felt suffocating. The light in the room seemed dimmer, the buzz of conversation quieter. People moved about the office, but there was a coldness in the air, a sense of distrust and uncertainty. It was as if the company had forgotten how to function without her.

Rhea glanced around. The staff, once excited and eager, seemed distant, their eyes avoiding hers as she walked past them. Some of the employees paused in their work, stealing glances at her, unsure whether to acknowledge her presence or not. She could feel their hesitation, but she didn’t let it faze her. She wasn’t here to apologize. She wasn’t here to explain herself. She was here to take back what was rightfully hers.

As she made her way deeper into the office, she finally came face to face with Vivaan. Their eyes met, and for a brief moment, time seemed to stop. The weight of everything unsaid—the months of separation, the missed opportunities, the unspoken feelings—hung between them, thick and undeniable. Neither of them spoke. Neither of them moved. The tension in the air was palpable, and yet neither of them knew what to say.

Vivaan’s face softened for a moment, but he quickly masked his emotions, turning away and walking past her without a word. Rhea’s heart beat a little faster, but she quickly pushed the feeling aside. This wasn’t the time for emotions. This was business.

As she continued walking, Rhea caught the eye of a few key members of the office—people who had worked with her before, who had been loyal to her during the early days. Their reactions were mixed, but one thing was clear: they respected her, even if they were unsure of what her return meant. Some looked at her with relief, others with wariness, unsure of how to navigate the dynamics that had shifted in her absence.

Rhea nodded at a few of them, a silent acknowledgement of their presence, but didn’t engage. She had a purpose here, and she was determined to fulfill it.

Walking up to her assistant’s desk, Rhea paused for a moment. Her assistant, a woman in her late twenties named Asha, looked up in surprise, her eyes wide as she stood up from her seat. The shock of seeing Rhea was clear in her expression, but she quickly composed herself.

“Good morning, Rhea ma’am.” Asha’s voice was tentative, but there was something new in the way she spoke—a new respect, perhaps.

Rhea gave her a small, confident smile. “I need an update on the board meeting,” she said smoothly, her voice clear and firm. “Is Dilip in there?”

Asha quickly nodded. “Yes, ma’am. He’s been in the meeting since the morning. The board’s discussing the quarterly results.” Her tone was hesitant, but it wasn’t fear—just unspoken understanding that this moment felt different.

“Good.” Rhea’s response was crisp, determined. “I’ll join them. Make sure the team is ready for a full review after the meeting.”

As Asha quickly nodded and went to make the necessary arrangements, Rhea stood still for a moment, looking around the office once more. This was it. She was back. There was no going back now. The future of the company was in her hands again, and she wasn’t going to let it slip away this time.

The boardroom was heavy with tension as the members shuffled into their seats. The sleek glass table gleamed under the overhead lights, but the coldness in the room was unmistakable. Rhea walked in, her heels clicking on the floor. Every eye turned toward her, and for a moment, she stood still, absorbing the weight of their gazes.

She walked toward the head of the table, sitting down in her usual seat, her posture straight, her gaze unwavering. Dilip Kapoor, her grandfather and the former CEO, sat across from her, arms crossed, his expression a mixture of disdain and frustration. The rest of the board members—some loyal, some neutral—waited for the inevitable confrontation.

Dilip cleared his throat and leaned forward. His voice was sharp, cutting through the silence.

Dilip: “Rhea, I see you’ve decided to come back, but I’m afraid you’re too late. The company’s market performance is dismal, and frankly, your leadership has been nothing short of disastrous. You’ve lost the trust of the board, and you’ve lost the trust of the investors. I’m not sure how you think you can just step in and take control again.”

Rhea’s eyes met his, but she didn’t flinch. Instead, her lips curled into a calm, controlled smile.

Rhea: “Grandfather, I never resigned. The position of CEO was never vacated. And as much as you might like to forget, this company was built by our family, and I’m the one entrusted to lead it now. The board’s decision still stands.”

Her voice remained steady, but there was an undeniable firmness in her tone, one that she had earned through the past few months of self-reflection and growth.

Dilip’s jaw tightened as he leaned forward, his gaze darkening.

Dilip: “I’ve heard your words, but the company doesn’t run on sentiment. It runs on results. And let’s not forget, you were the one who botched the last campaign—the company’s image plummeted because of your emotional decisions. You allowed the PR disaster to unfold. You don’t have the skillset to lead this company.”

A board member, Kunal Shah, shifted uncomfortably in his seat, trying to maintain a sense of neutrality but unable to stay silent.

Kunal: “Rhea, it’s not about sentiment, but the company’s financial position right now. The stock price is at an all-time low. We need stability, and with all due respect, Dilip’s methods have given us that—he’s been steering the ship through troubled waters.”

Rhea turned to Kunal, her expression neutral but firm.

Rhea: “Kunal, you’re right. The company does need stability, but we also need innovation and vision—things we’ve been lacking. Grandpa, with all due respect, has tried to hold on to methods that no longer serve the business. We need a fresh approach in this digital era, and I’m here to provide that. The company needs to adapt, not continue to follow outdated strategies.”

Her words rang through the room, and for a moment, the tension seemed to ease, though the skepticism in some members' eyes remained.

Dilip’s face twisted in anger as he pushed himself up from the table, his hands slamming down. He looked around at the board, eyes searching for any allies.

Dilip: “So now you think you have the knowledge to run this company? You think you can just walk in and fix the mess you’ve created? We’ve all seen your failures, Rhea. Your emotional leadership will only continue to pull this company deeper into the ground.”

He glanced at the board members, expecting support, but instead, he found only hesitant glances.

Dilip: “And now you expect me to just hand over the reins? You really think the shareholding power you’ve gained in your absence will be enough to back up your position?”

Rhea: “I don’t need anyone’s approval, grandfather. I’ve earned my place as CEO, and I intend to rebuild this company. You may have shareholding, but I have the trust of the people who actually run this company.”

Rhea stood up, the room now fully silent except for the sound of her voice as it echoed across the boardroom.

Rhea: “If you believe that corporate control is the only thing that matters here, then you’ve lost sight of what this company is about. Kapoor Fashion isn’t just about the numbers, it’s about legacy. And I’m the one who will carry it forward, not you.”

Dilip’s face turned a deep shade of red, his fists clenched at his sides. The air between them was electric, but Rhea stood her ground, unwavering.

Dilip: “You think I’m going to sit here and let you ruin everything my family has built? I can still stop this, Rhea. Don’t forget, I have the majority shareholding in Kapoor Fashion. I can take back control if I want to.”

Rhea’s gaze never wavered.

Rhea: “I don’t need your shares to run this company. If you think your power can overrule the future of Kapoor Fashion, then go ahead and try. But don’t mistake fear for respect.”

Dilip was silent for a long moment, his face twisted in frustration. He looked around, hoping for support from the board, but it was clear they were no longer on his side.

With a final, angry growl, Dilip stormed out of the room, slamming the door behind him. His voice echoed through the hallway.

Dilip: “This Company is doomed under her leadership. I’ll see to that.”

The boardroom was left in stunned silence, the weight of the moment settling over everyone. Some members exchanged glances, unsure of where this would lead, but it was clear: Rhea was back, and this time, she wasn’t going to let anyone stand in her way. After a while, Rhea pushed open the glass door to the meeting room, expecting to find it empty. But instead, Vivaan was already there.

He sat at the far end of the table, his posture slightly hunched over a notepad, scribbling something down in quick, focused strokes. His brows were drawn together, his fingers tapping lightly against the paper as if piecing together a puzzle only he understood.

It wasn’t work-related—at least, not in the way most people at Kapoor Fashion worked.

Whatever he was doing, he was doing it in secret.

Rhea took a step inside, and as the door clicked shut behind her, Vivaan immediately stiffened.

He looked up, his fingers instinctively flipping the notepad closed.

For a moment, neither of them spoke.

It was the first time they had been truly alone in months.

The silence stretched, thick with hesitation, unresolved words, and unspoken history.

Rhea’s fingers curled slightly at her sides. She wasn’t sure what to say, where to start.

Vivaan was the one who finally broke the silence.

"Didn’t expect you this early," he muttered, leaning back in his chair, as if trying to act indifferent.

Rhea crossed her arms, her gaze flickering to the notepad still clutched in his hand. “What were you writing?”

Vivaan didn’t answer right away. Instead, he exhaled sharply, then slid the notepad into his bag without a word. “Nothing important.

Rhea arched a brow. "You sure about that?"

Vivaan smirked, but it didn’t reach his eyes. “You’ve got enough to deal with already, Rhea. You don’t need to worry about what I’m doing.”

She didn’t push further. Not yet.

Instead, she walked toward the table, taking her seat across from him.

"You stayed," she said quietly, watching him carefully.

Vivaan hesitated for just a second before replying. “I made a promise.”

Something in her chest tightened at the simplicity of his answer.

Her gaze instinctively dropped to his hands—and that’s when she saw it.

A ring.

A simple, understated gold band on his left hand.

The sight of it sent a sharp jolt through her, though she kept her expression unreadable.

“You’re engaged.”

Vivaan followed her gaze to his ring. For a fraction of a second, something flickered across his face—hesitation? Regret? But he masked it too quickly for her to know for sure.

“Yeah,” he admitted. “A while ago, actually.”

Rhea nodded slowly, her voice measured. “She must be happy.”

Vivaan let out a quiet breath. “I guess.”

The answer was too vague. Too indifferent.

Something about it made Rhea’s stomach twist.

Before she could stop herself, she asked, “Is it what you wanted?”

Vivaan’s jaw tensed slightly. He didn’t answer immediately.

Finally, he exhaled, leaning forward slightly. “It’s what my family wanted.”

The weight behind his words settled between them like an invisible wall.

Rhea knew that feeling all too well.

She took a slow breath, deciding it was her turn to say what she had been holding in.

"I left because I felt like I had already lost," she admitted.

Vivaan’s eyes snapped back to hers, his brow furrowing slightly. “Lost what?”

She let out a quiet, humorless laugh. “Everything.”

She shook her head, as if trying to piece the words together. “I didn’t just fail at the PR campaign, Vivaan. I failed my father. I failed the company. I failed... myself.”

Vivaan remained silent, watching her carefully.

"You know what hurt the most?" she continued, voice softer now. "It wasn’t the media backlash. Or the board turning against me. It was my grandfather looking me in the eye and telling me that he had always known I wasn’t capable. That I was too emotional, too impulsive."

Vivaan's jaw tightened.

"I believed him," Rhea admitted. "I let those words sink into me like they were the truth. And instead of proving him wrong, I ran."

She exhaled, finally looking up at him. "I spent six months asking myself if I had made the right decision. If I should have fought harder. But when I saw the news… when I saw what was happening to Kapoor Fashion, I knew I had to come back."

Vivaan studied her for a long moment, then leaned back slightly. “And now?”

Rhea’s lips pressed together. “Now, I know I didn’t come back to prove him wrong. Or anyone else, for that matter.” She swallowed. “I came back for myself.”

Vivaan didn’t say anything at first. But there was something in his expression—understanding. Like he had known exactly what that felt like, too.

After a beat, he let out a quiet breath. “Well then… it’s about damn time.”

Rhea blinked, then let out a short, unexpected laugh.

The tension between them shifted—not gone, but momentarily lighter.

Vivaan smirked, shaking his head. “The company’s a mess, by the way,” he said, his voice carrying forced lightness. “I mean, I don’t get all the corporate lingo, but even I can tell Dilip made a huge mess.”

Rhea frowned. “How bad?”

Vivaan shrugged. “Bad. The investors are pulling out, the numbers are crashing, and the brand? It’s—” He paused, searching for the right word. “—disconnected. I mean, we’re supposed to be Kapoor Fashion, right? A legacy brand? But the marketing is cold. The campaigns feel forced. Everything about it feels… fake.”

Rhea listened, taking in his words. He didn’t use corporate terminology, but what he was saying was spot on.

“So, you’ve been trying to fix it?” she asked, tilting her head slightly.

Vivaan hesitated, then gave a small nod. “I’ve been working on something with the PR team. Nothing official, just… I had to try something. I didn’t want to watch it collapse.”

Rhea felt something stir in her chest—gratitude.

“You didn’t have to do that.” Her voice was quieter now, almost careful.

Vivaan looked at her then, really looked at her, and for a second—just a second—the world around them faded.

“I wanted to,” he admitted.

The words settled in the air between them, thick with something neither of them wanted to name.

And then—

The meeting room door swung open.

The team filed in, voices filling the once silent space.

Rhea and Vivaan stepped away from each other simultaneously, as if jolted back into reality.

The moment was gone.

But the feeling wasn’t.

The meeting room was packed. PR heads, marketing strategists, and creative directors sat around the long conference table, some with arms crossed, some taking notes, others simply observing. The tension in the air was unmistakable.

Rhea stood at the front, composed, unreadable. It had been months since she had been in this room, leading discussions, making decisions. But now, she was back.

And this time, she wasn’t here to seek approval.

She was here to lead.

"Let’s not waste time," Rhea began, her voice firm. "Kapoor Fashion’s image is in ruins. Our campaigns are failing, our audience isn’t connecting with us, and we’ve lost the trust of the people who made this brand what it is."

A few murmurs rippled through the team, some nodding in agreement, others exchanging glances.

"The problem is simple," she continued. "We’ve lost our authenticity. We’ve spent too long chasing a polished, unattainable image that no longer resonates. Kapoor Fashion was built on craftsmanship, on emotion, on storytelling. And that’s exactly what we’re going to bring back."

She tapped on the presentation screen behind her, and an image filled the display—a raw, unfiltered black-and-white photograph of a model mid-motion, laughter caught in the moment, the fabric of the dress flowing naturally.

"We’re returning to the raw look. But not just as a campaign. This will be our new identity."

The room fell silent.

"Kapoor Fashion will no longer be about unattainable perfection. It will be about real people, real moments, real emotions. From concept to execution, we will showcase everything—not just the final product, but the process behind it. The design trials, the fittings, the creative mess. The struggle and passion that go into making something extraordinary."

A flicker of something passed through the PR team’s eyes—something subtle but noticeable. Recognition. Satisfaction. A sense of finally getting what they had been waiting for.

But why they reacted that way, Rhea couldn’t quite tell.

She turned back to the room. "We’re going to show the world what Kapoor Fashion really is. Not a brand that hides behind glossy perfection, but one that thrives in its raw, artistic truth. This is the revival plan."

A beat of silence.

Then, Amit, one of the senior marketing strategists, leaned forward. “Didn’t We tried this and Failed!?”

It was a valid question.

Rhea didn’t hesitate.

“We failed because we weren’t in control of the narrative,” she stated, her voice steady. “Last time, we let the public dictate how the story unfolded. We let a single misinterpreted moment define us. This time, we own the story.”

She let the words settle before continuing.

“The difference isn’t just in execution. It’s in intention. We won’t just be ‘authentic’ for the sake of a campaign. Kapoor Fashion will redefine itself as a brand that thrives on transparency—on showing every step of the creative journey.”

She turned to the room, meeting their gazes one by one.

“We don’t just sell clothes. We sell the passion behind them. And that’s what we’re bringing back.”

The tension in the room didn’t fully disappear, but something shifted. Understanding. A slow nod from one team member, then another.

Finally, Amit exhaled, leaning back in his chair. “Alright. Let’s make this work.”

The meeting wrapped up, voices murmuring as people began filing out, discussing execution plans.

But Rhea didn’t move.

Neither did Vivaan.

As the last of the team members left the room, Rhea found herself standing near the table, Vivaan beside her, hands in his pockets.

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then, Vivaan broke the silence.

“Didn’t think you’d be back and taking over the world this fast,” he said, a teasing lilt to his voice.

Rhea let out a small chuckle, shaking her head. “I didn’t either.”

He looked at her then, really looked at her, and for a moment, the energy in the room shifted.

The professional lines between them blurred, just for a second.

Vivaan reached out instinctively, his fingers brushing against her forearm—not just a casual touch, but something that lingered a beat too long.

Rhea stiffened, her breath catching.

Neither of them pulled away immediately.

His touch was warm. Familiar. But different.

Something flickered in his eyes—something unspoken.

And then, just as quickly as it happened, he pulled back.

“Good job in there,” he said, his voice softer now.

Rhea nodded, but she barely registered the words.

The touch had been brief, but it left an impression.

She wasn’t sure what it meant.

And for now, she wasn’t ready to find out.

Rhea sat alone in her office, staring at the dimly lit city skyline through the glass window. The day had been long, exhausting, yet one moment kept replaying in her mind.

Vivaan’s touch.

It had been brief—just a brush against her arm—but it had lingered, unspoken, undeniable.

Why had it made her feel something?

She exhaled slowly, rubbing her temples. It wasn’t supposed to matter. He was engaged. That fact should have been enough to erase whatever this was before it even formed.

And yet…

Her fingers unconsciously traced the spot on her arm where his touch had been.

It wasn’t just the touch. It was his eyes in that moment—something raw, unguarded, as if for just a second, he had let himself feel something too.

Why am I thinking about this?

She shook her head, leaning back in her chair. It was ridiculous. There was no time for this—she had just gotten her company back.

But the way her heart had skipped in that moment…

That wasn’t something she could ignore.

Vivaan’s Thoughts – The Guilt That Shouldn’t Exist Across the city, Vivaan stood on his apartment balcony, gripping the cold metal railing. The air was cool against his skin, but his mind was burning with one thought.

What the hell happened back there?

It wasn’t just a touch.

It was a second too long. A hesitation too noticeable.

And now, it was lodged in his chest like a weight he couldn’t shake.

I’m engaged.

The words echoed in his head, loud, firm, like a fact he needed to remind himself of before this spiral went too far.

But if it was so simple… why was he still standing here, replaying the moment like it meant something?

He exhaled sharply, running a hand through his hair. This wasn’t supposed to happen. He wasn’t supposed to feel this.

He needed a distraction.

The plan.

Yes. The plan.

That was what mattered right now. That was what he should be focusing on.

But should he tell Rhea?

She deserved to know. She needed to know.

His fingers hovered over his phone, her name glowing on the screen.

But then he hesitated.

She had too much on her plate already—just returning, dealing with the board, fixing Kapoor Fashion. Would telling her now just complicate things?

He sighed and locked his phone. Not yet.

Closing Moment – Two Hearts, One Realization

At that same moment, Rhea’s gaze flickered to her reflection in the office glass.

She didn’t recognize this woman.

This wasn’t the same person who had walked away from Kapoor Fashion months ago.

She had changed.

And so had something between them.

Neither of them knew where this was going.

But for the first time, neither could deny that something had shifted.

And neither of them were ready for what that meant.
