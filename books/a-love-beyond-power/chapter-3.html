Chapter 3

The PR Plan

After weeks of working together, Rhea and Vivaan had settled into an unusual dynamic—one where sarcasm and professionalism blended seamlessly. Their conversations had taken a casual turn, an unspoken truce in the battlefield of deadlines and expectations.

One evening, as Rhea stood by the large windows of her office, staring at the endless cityscape, Vivaan strolled in, hands tucked into his pockets. "Tell me something," he began, leaning against the desk with a smirk. "Do CEOs ever take breaks, or is that considered treason?"

Rhea exhaled, shaking her head. "Breaks are for people who can afford mistakes. I don’t have that luxury."

Something in her tone made Vivaan pause. This wasn’t just about a marketing campaign for her. It was personal. The exhaustion in her posture, the weight behind her words—it was more than just pressure. It was the kind of burden someone carried when they had something to prove.

He studied her for a moment before speaking. "You know, Rhea, this campaign—it's really important to you, isn’t it?"

Rhea hesitated for a second before responding, her voice quieter than before. "Yes. It is."

Vivaan didn’t push further, but he nodded slightly, as if he understood something unspoken. "Then let’s make sure it works."

Vivaan made a dramatic show of looking around. "Huh. No cameras, no press. Just us. You can say it—you secretly enjoy my interruptions."

She shot him a sideways glance. "Let me guess. You’re here to enlighten me with some newfound wisdom?"

"Oh, absolutely," he said, grabbing one of the untouched files from her desk and flipping it open as if he had any idea what it contained. "For starters, maybe don’t bore yourself to death with these numbers."

"You don’t even know what you’re looking at," she countered.

"Nope," he admitted. "But I do know that numbers alone don’t sell a brand. Stories do. And right now, Kapoor Fashion is all numbers and no story."

That got her attention. She turned to face him fully, crossing her arms. "You think we don’t have a story?"

He set the file down. "Not one people care about. You’re selling a fantasy—perfect clothes, perfect models, perfect campaigns. But tell me, when was the last time you saw people connect with perfection?"

She narrowed her eyes slightly. "Go on."

"What if we scrap the polished image?" He pushed himself off the desk, walking toward her. "Drop the luxury gloss and show people the real Kapoor Fashion—the late nights, the stress, the breakthroughs. The real people behind the brand."

Rhea arched a brow. "You mean expose our chaos?"

"I mean humanize it." He held her gaze. "A behind-the-scenes campaign. Raw footage. No scripted ads, no models posing under artificial lights. Just real moments—designers at work, creative blocks, last-minute fixes. The audience sees the struggle, the passion, the effort. We let them feel like they’re part of something."

She tapped her fingers against her arm, processing. It was a risk. A huge one. But...

Her gut told her it could work.

"Do you believe in this idea?" she asked.

Vivaan smirked. "I believe in good storytelling. And this? This could make Kapoor Fashion feel alive."

After a long pause, Rhea exhaled. "Fine. Let’s do it."

A slow grin spread across Vivaan’s face. "You’re trusting me?"

She rolled her eyes. "I’m trusting my instincts. Don’t make me regret it."

He chuckled. "No pressure at all."

But as she turned back to her desk, she caught his reflection in the glass—his easy confidence, the way he watched her like he’d already known she’d say yes.

And for the first time, she wondered if she wasn’t just trusting his idea—but him.

The next morning, with Rhea’s silent approval, Vivaan presented the idea in the strategy meeting. He was met with skepticism at first, but Rhea’s backing made people listen.

“We’ve done perfection,” she told them. “Now, let’s show them reality.”

The shift was happening. And for the first time, Vivaan felt like he wasn’t just working for Rhea—he was working with her.

The Kapoor Fashion office was nearly silent, the usual hum of activity replaced by the occasional flicker of the overhead lights. The city skyline stretched beyond the glass windows, its lights twinkling like distant stars. The world outside was asleep, but inside, two people remained—Rhea Kapoor and Vivaan Mehra.

Vivaan stretched his arms, letting out a low groan. "You know, I’m starting to think you have an agenda to keep me here past midnight every day. Is this part of my job description? Because if so, I demand overtime."

Rhea, still focused on the campaign outline in front of her, barely spared him a glance. "Your contract says ‘whatever it takes.’ So no, no overtime."

He scoffed, leaning back in his chair. "So cruel. And here I thought CEOs were supposed to treat their employees with kindness."

"I didn’t hire you for kindness," she shot back, scribbling notes onto a document. "I hired you to make this campaign work."

Vivaan watched her for a moment, the way the dim light softened her usually sharp features. The exhaustion in her posture was clear—she had been running on sheer willpower for days. He reached for his coffee cup only to realize it was empty.

He frowned. "You need a break."

"We don’t have time for breaks," Rhea replied automatically, flipping to the next page.

Vivaan shook his head and stood up. "No, what you don’t have is a functioning brain if you think you can run on nothing but stress and caffeine."

She sighed, rubbing her temple. "Vivaan—"

Before she could argue, he slid his own cup toward her. "Here. It’s cold, but hey, adds to the ‘real and raw’ aesthetic of our campaign, right?"

Rhea stared at the cup for a second before hesitantly taking it. "You’re impossible."

"And yet, you haven’t fired me." He leaned against the table, grinning. "That says something."

She rolled her eyes but didn’t respond. Instead, she took a sip, her eyes scanning the campaign notes again.

After a few quiet moments, Vivaan spoke. "You know, we should add this to the campaign."

Rhea looked up. "What?"

He gestured around the room. "This. The late-night struggle. The behind-the-scenes chaos. The CEO who works past midnight because she gives a damn. Show people what leadership really looks like."

Rhea blinked, caught off guard. "You want to film… me?"

"Not just you. Everyone. But yeah, you too," he said, holding her gaze. "People connect with what’s real. They need to see what goes into making Kapoor Fashion what it is—not just the finished product. The real work, the stress, the passion. You."

She tapped her pen against the desk, thinking. "It’s risky."

Vivaan smirked. "So’s trusting a photographer with your company’s image. But here we are."

She let out a small chuckle, shaking her head. "I don’t know, Vivaan. This has to work."

Something in her voice made him pause. He leaned forward, resting his elbows on the desk. "You keep saying that. But you never say why."

Rhea hesitated before finally speaking. “My grandfather thinks I can’t handle this. That I’ll ruin Kapoor Fashion. If this campaign fails… maybe he’s right.”

Vivaan’s playful smirk faded. He held her gaze, voice steady. “He’s wrong.”

She let out a short laugh, but it lacked amusement. “And how would you know?”

“Because I’ve seen you work.” He shrugged. “You don’t half-ass anything. You push harder than anyone here. And if you need this campaign to prove it? Then we make it the biggest success Kapoor Fashion has ever seen.”

She watched him, something unreadable in her eyes.

“Big words for a photographer,” she murmured.

Vivaan leaned in slightly, his voice teasing but firm. “I never make promises I can’t keep.”

She looked at him, searching his face for hesitation. There was none.

A beat of silence passed between them, the quiet stretching out, charged.

Vivaan reached for a fabric sample on the table at the same time Rhea did, and their fingers brushed—light, fleeting, but undeniably noticeable.

Neither of them moved away.

Rhea’s breath hitched slightly, but she didn’t pull back. Neither did Vivaan. The weight of the moment settled between them, something shifting, something unspoken making itself known.

He was the first to break the silence, his voice softer now. "Rhea…"

She didn’t respond, but she didn’t look away either. Her fingers remained against his, the warmth of his touch a stark contrast to the cold coffee in her other hand.

For a second, it felt like the entire world had narrowed to this—the two of them, alone in a room, with nothing but the quiet tension and the weight of something unspoken.

Then, just as suddenly as it happened, Rhea pulled her hand away, clearing her throat. "We should wrap this up."

Vivaan exhaled, nodding, but there was something different in his expression now—something unreadable. "Sure, boss."

She turned away, but not before catching his reflection in the window. His gaze was still on her, thoughtful, unreadable.

And for the first time, she wondered if maybe… she wasn’t just trusting his ideas anymore. Maybe she was trusting him.
