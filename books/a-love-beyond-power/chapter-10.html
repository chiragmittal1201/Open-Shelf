Chapter 10

Breaking the Barriers

Rhea stood by the glass window of her office, the city lights stretching endlessly before her. The press conference had been a success—far beyond what she had imagined. The campaign was already making waves, and for the first time, her grandfather had acknowledged her leadership in front of everyone. The weight she had carried for so long, the constant need to prove herself, felt a little lighter.

But how should she celebrate this?

A grand party? No.

Dinner with colleagues? Not enough.

Something intimate… but with whom?

Her gaze flickered across the office and landed on Vivaan.

He was seated near the desk, flipping through some files, his sleeves rolled up, exposing his forearms. He looked relaxed, but she could see it—the subtle pride in his expression, the way his lips curled slightly upward, the way he kept stealing glances at her, as if knowing exactly what this moment meant to her.

Vivaan had been there through it all.

Her heart swelled. She wanted to celebrate with him. Just him.

Without giving herself time to rethink, she turned, walking straight towards him.

"Vivaan," she called.

He looked up, raising a brow, sensing something in her tone. "Yes, boss?" he teased, leaning back in his chair.

She folded her arms, pretending to be annoyed. "Don’t call me that."

"What? Boss? But you are my boss," he smirked, clearly enjoying himself.

Rhea rolled her eyes. "Forget it. I was going to invite you for a small celebration, but since you’re so busy teasing me—"

His smirk disappeared. "Wait, wait—celebration? What kind of celebration?"

She hesitated for a moment before answering, "At my place. Just us. I… I want to celebrate this properly, and I want to thank you too."

Something in the air shifted.

Vivaan sat up straight, studying her, as if trying to read between the lines. "Are you sure you want it to be just us?"

"Yes."

The way she said it—firm, certain—made his chest tighten.

He exhaled, giving her a long look before nodding. "Okay then. Your place it is."

A strange excitement buzzed in the air between them. Neither of them acknowledged it, but they both felt it.

In the evening the faint chime of the doorbell echoed through the quiet apartment.

Vivaan stood at the doorstep, straightening the collar of his shirt—an indigo blue, crisp and perfectly fitted, with the sleeves rolled just enough to give him an effortless charm. His expression was unreadable at first, perhaps hiding a flicker of nervousness. But before he could dwell on it, the door opened—not to Rhea, but to a neatly dressed house help.

"Please come in, sir," the help said with a polite smile, stepping aside.

Vivaan entered, his gaze casually sweeping the room—until it landed on her.

And then... everything stilled.

Rhea stood at the far end of the living room, a soft, welcoming smile on her lips, completely unaware of how she looked like she had stepped straight out of a painting.

Her dress was a flowing rust-colored masterpiece, adorned with delicate white floral patterns that cascaded across the fabric like a painter’s careful brushstrokes. The deep V-neckline added a touch of understated elegance, while the fluttering cap sleeves softened her shoulders, lending her a fragile, almost ethereal grace. A thin, adjustable tie cinched at her waist, shaping her silhouette with quiet confidence. The material moved with her—light, fluid, almost like it danced before she did.

Vivaan blinked once. Then again.

"Whoa..." he whispered under his breath.

Rhea tilted her head slightly. “What?”

He chuckled softly, hands resting casually in his pockets, though his eyes gave away more than he intended. “I just realized... if someone asked me what poetry in motion looks like, I’d point in your direction.”

Her cheeks turned a warm pink, and she let out a tiny laugh, rolling her eyes. “Drama suits you.”

Vivaan grinned. “No, really. You look... incredible.”

Before the moment could grow too electric, the help interrupted with a gentle voice, “Ma’am, dinner is ready.”

Rhea nodded, glancing toward the dining area. “Shall we?”

Vivaan followed, his mind still stuck somewhere between the doorway and that first look at her, a strange tension already humming quietly beneath his cool exterior.

The dining table was modest but tastefully arranged—warm lights, two wine glasses, and plates already served with Vivaan’s favorite: paneer lababdar, soft garlic naan, jeera rice, and a small bowl of gulab jamuns waiting quietly at the side.

Vivaan settled into his seat, eyeing the food with a touch of surprise.

“You remembered,” he said, lifting a brow.

Rhea smiled, reaching for the serving spoon. “Of course. You said this once during that horrible offsite lunch—you know, the one with the cardboard-tasting sandwiches.”

He chuckled. “I did. And then I ranted for fifteen minutes about how underrated paneer lababdar is.”

“You said it's the dish of romantics with a spicy past,” she quoted, laughing.

Vivaan grinned. “Still true.”

They began eating, talking about random things at first—how chaotic the press conference had been behind the scenes, how the marketing team kept messing up the cue cards, how the media had flooded the email with interview requests.

“Feels weird, doesn’t it?” Rhea said, picking at her naan. “That people are finally starting to believe in this... in me.”

Vivaan looked at her gently. “You earned it. You didn’t just show up—you bled for this.”

Rhea’s eyes softened for a second before she looked down again. “Still. I’m not used to being praised.”

Vivaan leaned in slightly. “Then get used to it. Because tonight, I’m not letting you off the hook without at least five compliments.

She laughed, nudging his foot under the table. “Cocky.”

He tilted his head. “Honest.”

The help quietly walked in, her phone pressed to her ear, her expression tense.

“Ma’am,” she said softly, turning to Rhea. “I’m really sorry, but my son’s fever is back. My husband’s with him, but I think I should go home.”

“Oh God, of course. Please, go,” Rhea said immediately, standing. “Don’t worry about anything here.”

“I’ve set everything on the side. You both just enjoy.” The help gave a small bow and hurried out the door.

As the latch clicked shut, a sudden hush fell over the apartment. The city buzzed faintly outside the window, but inside—it was just the two of them, with the golden light flickering against the wine.

Rhea took another bite, slower this time. Her heart felt full... but restless.

Why am I always this happy around him? she wondered. Is it the way he listens? The way he makes everything feel like it’ll be okay?

She looked up at him, and for a second, forgot how to swallow.

Vivaan, on the other hand, wasn’t eating anymore. His fork sat untouched. He was staring at her—not in a way that demanded attention, but the kind of stare that came when you weren’t aware you were staring at all.

What am I doing? he thought. She’s beautiful, yes—but it’s not just that. It’s everything. The way she tilts her head when she’s curious. The way her eyes fight back tears and win. Why does she feel like home in a place that’s not mine?

He looked away quickly, focusing on his glass.

But something had shifted. Unspoken, yet heavy between them.

And neither of them truly wanted to go back to before.

The soft music playing in the background wasn’t meant to fill the silence—it was just there, part of the room, part of the mood. A slow instrumental melody floated through the apartment, warm and unhurried, like it had nowhere else to be.

Vivaan leaned back in his chair, fingers curled around his glass. “This music... it's got that old school charm. Reminds me of something my mom used to play on Sundays.”

Rhea looked up from her glass, curious. “Classical?”

He shook his head, a faint smile tugging at his lips. “Waltz, I think. She was obsessed with ballroom dancing. Said it was the most romantic form of expression ever created. She used to pull my dad into dancing around the kitchen.”

Rhea grinned, warmth flickering in her chest. “That’s adorable.”

There was a small pause. The music shifted—gentle violin now, almost coaxing.

“I’m sure you’ve never danced like that,” she teased, tilting her head.

Vivaan raised an eyebrow, leaning in slightly. “Are you challenging me, Ms. Kapoor?”

Her eyes sparkled. “Maybe I am.”

He stood, mock serious. “Then allow me to defend my dignity.”

Rhea laughed and stood with him, instinctively smoothing her dress. She didn’t realize how breathtaking she looked until she saw Vivaan falter for half a second—his gaze lingering, caught.

There it is again, she thought. That look.

He extended a hand. “May I have this dance?”

She took it, without hesitation.

The room shifted.

What started as a playful moment—a joke carried too far—became something else.

Their hands fit together too well. Their bodies aligned with an ease neither could explain. The steps were soft, swaying in rhythm with the music and the faint city glow pouring in from the balcony.

A gentle spin.

A laugh muffled by the closeness.

His hand at her waist. Her fingers brushing his collar. Their eyes—locked.

The music slowed.

So did they.

The laughter faded, replaced by something deeper. The music no longer felt like background. It pulsed between them, syncing with their breath. Rhea's hand lingered a little longer against his chest. His fingers tightened slightly at her waist.

They were close. Too close.

She tilted her head ever so slightly, her eyes dipping to his lips for a fraction of a second.

I’m really going to kiss him, she thought. And I want to. Why do I want to?

Vivaan didn’t move.

Not away.

Not forward.

But his body didn’t listen—not at first. His hand brushed against the bare skin of her upper back where the dress dipped, and she shivered—not from the cold, but from the sudden electricity.

Their faces were inches apart.

Seconds.

A breath.

Then—he blinked hard.

Pulled back.

Just enough.

His voice was low, strained. “I should go.”

Rhea stood frozen, lips parted, heart racing. “Vivaan—”

But he didn’t look back.

He stepped away quickly, grabbed his coat from the back of the chair, and was out the door within seconds.

The music kept playing.

And Rhea just stood there.

One step away from something that could’ve changed everything.

The city blurred past Vivaan as he drove, but his mind was far from the road.

“Why did I come like this?”

He gripped the steering wheel tighter, jaw clenched. “I must have made Rhea upset. But what was I supposed to do? If I had stayed… if that had happened…”

He exhaled sharply, as if trying to breathe out the weight in his chest.

“No. I’m engaged. I shouldn’t have even let it come close. I shouldn’t have looked at her like that. Touched her like that.”

The memory of her fingertips against his, the way her eyes had closed in that second before the kiss—it clung to him like humidity in the air.

“But leaving… leaving was the right thing to do… right?”

He swallowed hard, eyes scanning the empty stretch of road ahead, yet completely lost.

“Was it? Then why do I feel like a coward?”

His foot eased on the accelerator.

“I didn’t imagine that moment. I felt it. We both did. But why was I about to… do that?”

His voice faltered in the quiet of the car. He wasn’t just asking the night or the city. He was asking himself.

“Do I… have feelings for her?”

The thought wasn’t new. But tonight, it echoed louder than ever before.

And for the first time… he didn’t deny it.

Vivaan’s fingers tapped restlessly against the steering wheel as the signal turned red. The car stopped, but his mind raced.

“I have to end this confusion. I can’t pretend anymore. I need to figure this out.”

Rhea stood frozen in the middle of her quiet apartment, the music still humming softly in the background, long after Vivaan had left.

The air felt different now. Heavy. Still laced with the warmth of his presence and the weight of the moment that almost was.

Her fingers brushed against her lips.

What… what was I about to do?

Her breath caught. She turned slowly, her eyes searching the room like it might offer her answers.

I was really about to do that…

A soft laugh escaped her lips—half disbelief, half self-mockery. She sank onto the couch, her dress flowing around her like rust-colored silk waves.

Did Vivaan feel it too?

The way he looked at her. The way his fingers curled just a little tighter around hers during the dance. The way he froze in that last heartbeat before pulling away.

It wasn’t in her head. She was sure of that.

And still...

Why was I like that?

Why did I let myself get carried away like that?

I embarrassed myself, didn’t I?

She hid her face in her hands.

What if he thinks I was trying to seduce him?

That I was being reckless or desperate?

But that wasn’t it.

That’s not what this is. This wasn’t about the dance or the dress or the mood.

It was him.

It was always him.

Why… why am I like this around Vivaan?

Why does he always make me feel like I’m... losing control?

No, not losing control—feeling alive. That’s the word.

He made her feel seen. Heard. Understood. Like she wasn’t just the CEO fighting to prove herself, or the granddaughter trying to live up to a legacy—but just Rhea. Raw and real.

Every time he looked at her, it felt like the world paused. Every conversation sparked something inside her. Every silence between them felt like a song waiting to be sung.

Do I…

She paused, staring at nothing, and whispered into the silence:

“…Do I love him?”

It felt terrifying to say it. But also strangely… right.

All this time, it was there—lurking in glances, in late-night phone calls, in the way her heart felt just a little lighter when he was around.

Not just admiration. Not just friendship.

Love.

Pure. Unexpected. Unspoken.

And maybe—just maybe—not one-sided.

She smiled softly to herself, eyes shimmering.

“I love him.”

Vivaan sat behind the wheel, the engine humming quietly as the city’s night lights passed him by. But his mind—his heart—hadn’t moved an inch since he left Rhea’s apartment.

“Why did I leave like that?” he thought, gripping the steering wheel tighter. “Why couldn’t I look her in the eyes and just… stay?”

He’d been running, and he knew it. Running from a truth that was becoming harder to ignore.

“I wasn’t just about to kiss her… I wanted to.”

That realization didn’t come with panic. It came with a sense of inevitability. He had been telling himself it was wrong, that it wasn’t supposed to happen. But in that moment—with her in his arms, her scent in the air, her heartbeat so close to his—nothing had ever felt more right.

And now, he was here.

His car rolled to a stop in front of her building again. He stared up at the soft lights spilling through the windows of her home, a quiet warmth against the dark night.

He sat there for a full minute. Not moving. Not breathing, it felt like.

“What if I’ve already lost her by walking away?”

He got out slowly, walking toward the entrance like each step weighed something. When he reached her door, he didn’t ring the bell immediately. He stood there, eyes closed for a second, letting everything sink in.

“I don’t know what’s going to happen after tonight,” he whispered to himself, “but I know I can’t walk away again. Not without saying what I feel. Not without giving her—giving us—a chance.”

And with that, his finger pressed the doorbell.

The room was still, bathed in the soft glow of warm ceiling lights. Rhea sat curled up on the sofa, legs tucked to the side, eyes fixed on the untouched glass of water on the coffee table. The silence was no longer comforting. It was full—of things felt but unspoken. Her rust-colored dress flowed gently over her frame, its delicate fabric catching the ambient light in soft pulses as she shifted her weight.

And then—

Ding-dong.

The sudden chime of the doorbell jolted her upright.

Her heart gave a single, heavy thump.

She wasn’t expecting anyone. The help had left hours ago. She sat frozen for a beat too long, like her body was trying to catch up with the possibility that maybe—

No. It couldn’t be.

But she got up anyway. Her bare feet moved soundlessly across the floor, and with every step, the air thickened. As she approached the door, her fingers trembled—not from fear, but from something deeper… a hope she wasn’t ready to admit yet.

She opened the door slowly.

Vivaan.

He stood there, a shadow of restraint behind his eyes, his lips slightly parted as if the words were right there—but tangled in hesitation. He looked as though he’d been standing there for longer than he should have, battling whatever storm had brought him back.

Rhea blinked, her lips parting instinctively. But no words came.

Vivaan finally exhaled.

“Can we talk?”

Her breath caught. For a split second, she didn’t know whether to step back, or forward. Her eyes met his—quiet, searching, uncertain—and something within her softened.

She nodded slowly. “Yeah… yes,” she corrected, voice barely above a whisper. “Of course.”

She stepped aside, and Vivaan entered, his steps careful like he didn’t want to disturb the air between them.

The door clicked shut behind them.

But neither of them moved further.

Rhea stood near the door, arms loosely crossed in front of her, suddenly very aware of how close he was. The rust-colored fabric of her dress whispered against her skin with every tiny shift in her posture. She didn’t speak—she couldn’t. Her throat was tight, her mind louder than it had ever been.
