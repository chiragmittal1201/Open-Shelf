Chapter 12

The Cost of Love

The room was quiet, the kind of silence that lingered even in breath. A long mahogany table stretched through the center, polished to perfection, surrounded by a few chosen individuals. The heavy curtains blocked out most of the sunlight, giving the space an air of solemn gravity.

Rhea sat quietly, dressed in understated black, her face still, composed. Beside her sat Dilip Kapoor, her grandfather — sharp in his crisp suit, unmoved in expression, but not untouched. Grief didn’t always arrive in tears. Sometimes, it sat quietly behind unreadable eyes.

The lawyer flipped open the folder.

“As per the wishes of the late Mr. Aditya Kapoor,” he began, “all personal assets, including his shares in Kapoor Fashions, shall be inherited in full by his daughter, Ms. Rhea Kapoor.”

No reaction from Rhea — not even a flicker of surprise. She hadn’t come here expecting anything except the weight of her father’s absence.

“However,” the lawyer continued, voice steady, “as per company bylaws, the transfer of Mr. Kapoor’s shares will still require formal approval by Mr. Dilip Kapoor, as co-founder and senior-most shareholder.”

“Regarding the role of Chief Executive Officer,” he added, “Mr. Aditya Kapoor had expressed his desire for Rhea Kapoor to lead Kapoor Fashions permanently. However, her appointment remains subject to majority approval from the company board.”

Rhea’s hands remained folded. Her expression didn’t shift. But to her left, Dilip’s jaw clenched ever so slightly.

A pause followed. The lawyer glanced up, closing the file gently. “That concludes the formal reading.”

Chairs scraped softly as everyone began to rise. Dilip stood without a word. His face gave nothing away, but a sharp glint lingered in his eyes — not joy, not anger. Just something calculating. Something forming.

Rhea walked out of the room, her steps quiet on the marble floor.

Outside, the corridor stood empty except for one figure — Vivaan, waiting patiently, dressed in simple black, his gaze lifting the second she stepped out. She didn’t speak. She simply walked into his arms, burying herself in the only space that felt warm today.

He held her close, his chin resting softly against her head. No words. No questions.

Behind them, Dilip stepped out of the room.

He stopped.

Watched.

And said nothing.

Dilip Kapoor stood just beyond the threshold, half in shadow, watching as Rhea collapsed into Vivaan’s arms. Her fingers clutched his shirt like it was the only thing anchoring her to the earth, her head buried in his chest. Grief clung to her like a second skin.

Vivaan held her silently — not consoling her, not offering shallow words — just being there, letting her break.

Dilip’s jaw tightened. He didn’t speak. Didn’t move. But his gaze lingered on them, unreadable… until something behind his eyes shifted — a flicker of memory rising, unwanted, unstoppable.

Just days ago…

The hospital corridor had smelled like antiseptic and dread. Nurses spoke in hushed tones. The air was still, like it knew time had paused to make space for goodbyes.

Dilip had been sitting by the bed, unmoving, as machines beeped their fragile rhythm beside his son Aditya Kapoor. His son. His pride. His disappointment. His reflection.

Then the door burst open.

“Papa!” Rhea’s voice cracked as she rushed in, her footsteps frantic. Behind her, Vivaan followed, breathless, but respectful, keeping distance.

Aditya turned his head weakly toward the sound. A flicker of life sparked in his tired eyes.

“You made it,” he rasped, lips curling into the gentlest smile. “Always dramatic, just like your mother.”

Rhea let out a strangled sob and ran to him, dropping beside the bed and hugging him like a child again — fiercely, desperately.

“Don’t say that, Papa… please don’t say it like it’s over.”

“Shhh,” he whispered, hand stroking her hair. “It’s not over. Not yet. I had to see you. I had to see the girl who made the whole world listen… and the boy who makes her laugh.”

He glanced toward Vivaan, whose eyes flicked nervously to Dilip, then back.

“Vivaan Mehra, sir,” he said quietly.

Aditya gave a faint chuckle. “So formal. Rhea, is he scared of me?”

She laughed through her tears, forehead still pressed to his chest.

“A little,” she murmured.

“Good,” Aditya breathed. “He should be.”

For a second, the heaviness in the room lifted — not gone, just eased. Dilip watched from the side, arms folded. His face was stone, but his fingers twitched slightly. A blink lingered too long. The weight of this goodbye pressed on him too… but he swallowed it whole.

Aditya’s hand brushed his daughter’s cheek.

“Rhea… you’ve already become more than I ever dreamed. You turned that company into a story people believed in again.”

Rhea’s tears flowed harder.

“You’re the best CEO it’s ever had,” he whispered. “Even better than me.”

Then his breath hitched.

A long silence.

The monitor shrieked once… then turned flat.

Rhea froze.

Her scream echoed through the hospital walls — raw, guttural, and devastating. She clung to him, shaking, sobbing uncontrollably. Vivaan rushed forward and pulled her into his arms, letting her cry against his chest, burying her in safety.

Dilip didn’t move.

But his hands clenched. His throat tightened. He didn’t cry — but his silence was heavier than sound. His eyes blinked slower. Once. Twice. A moment passed. His son was gone.

Back in the present...

The corridor returned, brightly lit and unbearably quiet. Dilip watched as Rhea buried herself into Vivaan’s arms, just like that day.

He exhaled, slow and long.

Then he walked forward.

Rhea looked up, startled. Her face was still wet, her expression bare.

For a moment, Dilip simply looked at her — really looked. And then, in the softest voice he’d used in years, he said:

“Come home sometime, beta. We should talk.”

It wasn’t a command.

It was… something else.

Rhea nodded slowly, still holding onto Vivaan’s hand. She didn’t speak, but her eyes answered.

Dilip gave one small nod — almost imperceptible — and turned away.

But not before Vivaan noticed the smallest flicker of grief in the old man’s gaze.

A father had lost his son.

And a grandfather had just realized his granddaughter was no longer a girl — but a woman who stood for what she loved.

The next day, Sanya stood at the entrance of her freshly opened office, a calm smile painted over subtle tension. The nameplate read Mind Bridge – Emotional Wellness by Sanya. When she saw Vivaan, she straightened.

Sanya (smiling politely):

“Welcome… come in. Have a seat.”

Vivaan nodded, stepping inside and settling into the warm-toned room across from her.

Sanya (lightly teasing):

“You missed the inauguration, by the way.”

Vivaan (guilty smile):

“I know. I’m sorry. Things were… intense.”

Sanya (raising a brow):

“Too intense to show up for your fiancée?”

Vivaan sighed, not looking away this time.

Sanya (softer now):

“Where were you, Vivaan?”

Vivaan (honest):

“With Rhea. She needed me.”

There was a pause. Sanya absorbed that, her composure flickering just slightly.

Sanya (voice tightening):

“She needed you… and I didn’t? I’m the one you’re engaged to—not her.”

Vivaan didn’t flinch. He looked her in the eye.

Vivaan:

“I love her.”

Silence. No dramatic gasp. Just stillness.

Sanya (nodding slowly):

“I guess… I knew this was coming. Somewhere inside, I did.”

She stood and walked slowly to the window, arms folded, her back to him for a second before turning again—stronger now.

Sanya:

“Anyway, I’ve found something for myself too. That’s why I started this office. To help people like me… like us—who get lost trying to meet expectations, unsure of their own voice.”

Vivaan stood too. Not out of guilt—but respect.

Sanya (offering a faint smile):

“So… this is it, right?”

Vivaan (gently):

“This is it.”

She extended her hand. He took it.

Sanya:

“Take care, Vivaan.”

Vivaan:

“You too, Sanya.” They held that gaze a moment longer—then let go.

No bitterness. Just truth.

And Sanya was thinking

So this is how it ends. Not with drama… not with anger. Just quiet honesty.

I should feel broken, shouldn’t I? But I don’t. Not completely. Maybe because some part of me always knew I wasn’t the one he’d choose when it really mattered. Maybe because I never truly chose him either—I just accepted him.

But this pain… it’s still real. Not because I lost a man, but because I finally faced the truth.

Still… there’s something freeing in this moment. Like I just unclenched a fist I didn’t know I’d been holding.

He loved someone else, and I… I found something I want to build on my own. Something mine.

Goodbye, Vivaan. And thank you… for not lying to me.

The study was quiet—suffocating in its silence. The antique clock ticked with gentle authority, and the scent of old books and cedar wood hung in the air. Dilip Kapoor stood by the window, hands behind his back, watching the fading light of the evening.

Rhea sat across from him, silent. Grief still clung to her eyes like shadows refusing to lift.

Dilip turned finally, his expression unreadable but softer than usual.

Dilip:

“How are you doing, Rhea?”

She didn’t respond immediately. Just offered a faint nod.

Dilip:

“I know it’s not easy… losing a father. But Aditya wouldn’t want you to drown in this. He’d want you to carry forward… with your head held high, like he always did. Like you’re doing now.”

Rhea’s throat tightened, but she held herself steady. He approached slowly, sitting across from her.

Dilip (gently):

“You’ve done well. Better than anyone expected. Better than even your father, I’ll admit that.”

He gave a pause, allowing that rare compliment to land.

“The numbers you’ve delivered… they’re record-breaking. You made Kapoor Industries rise higher than ever. You should be proud.”

She looked up, offering a ghost of a smile. “Thank you, Dadu.”

A moment of quiet pride lingered… before it broke.

Dilip (sighing, slower now):

“Which is why I need to talk to you… about Vivaan.”

Rhea’s body stilled.

Dilip:

“I saw him outside the will reading. I saw how he held you.”

He didn’t say it with anger—just disappointment that cut deeper.

“You’re not just any woman, Rhea. You’re a Kapoor. You lead an empire. And yet… you stand beside a man who doesn’t even understand the world you’re in.”

Rhea (quiet, but firm):

“He may not understand your world, Dadu… but he understands me.”

Dilip:

“And what will that give you? A house in a middle-income neighborhood? Staff that call in sick every week? A man who’ll be looked down on in every boardroom you enter?”

Rhea (a little stronger):

“He stood by me when the company was at war. When I lost Papa. When I was falling apart, he didn’t run. He’s not from your world, but that doesn’t make him lesser.”

Dilip (clipped):

“Rhea—”

Rhea (interrupting softly):

“He’s not beneath me. And if you saw him the way I do, you’d know… he’s stronger than most men I’ve met in your world.”

Dilip leaned back, exhaling slowly. Then, after a moment:

Dilip:

“Maybe. But I’m not just your grandfather, Rhea. I’m also a guardian of this legacy.”

A pause.

Dilip (cold, final):

“If you want my approval—for the shares, and for the CEO confirmation from the board—then you will not marry that man.”

Rhea’s breath caught.

Dilip (softer, almost apologetic):

“This is not punishment. It’s protection. You are Dilip Kapoor’s granddaughter. I cannot let you throw yourself away.”

The silence that followed felt thick enough to drown in.

Rhea didn’t respond—not yet. Her fingers curled around the armrest, but her eyes didn’t leave her grandfather.

The kettle whistled faintly. A soft jazz melody played from Vivaan’s phone speaker, barely audible over the sizzle of vegetables in the pan. The curtains were drawn halfway, letting sunlight dapple the hardwood floor of Rhea’s kitchen.

Vivaan moved around with quiet ease, barefoot, wearing a plain black T-shirt and grey cotton track pants—clearly at home, though the house wasn’t his.

He stirred the pan one last time and turned the gas off. Just as he wiped his hands, his phone buzzed. He picked it up, already smiling.

Vivaan:

“Hi, Ma…”

His voice was softer, warmer.

Vivaan’s Mom (teasing):

“So you remember you have a mother, huh?”

Vivaan (grinning):

“Come on, I called you just yesterday.”

Vivaan’s Mom:

“Hmm. And are you eating properly or just feeding that CEO of yours while forgetting yourself?”

Vivaan (laughs, then sincere):

“I’m taking care of myself. And her too. She’s… she’s not doing great, Ma.”

A pause.

Vivaan’s Mom (gently):

“You love her, don’t you?”

Vivaan nodded, though she couldn’t see it.

Vivaan:

“Yes.”

Vivaan’s Mom (softly):

“She’s lucky. I’m happy for you… really. Just—Sanya was a sweet girl. I hope you told her properly.”

Vivaan (quietly):

“I did. It wasn’t easy… but it was honest.”

There was silence for a moment. Then she sighed.

Vivaan’s Mom:

“Okay. That’s all a mother wants to hear.”

Just then, the door clicked open.

Rhea entered, her face pale and drawn, her eyes still slightly red from crying earlier.

Vivaan turned, instinctively putting his phone down.

Vivaan (gently):

“Hey. You’re home.”

Rhea offered a weak nod, slipping her shoes off. She looked exhausted.

He didn’t ask her what happened. Instead, he walked over to the kitchen counter.

Vivaan:

“I made lunch. Nothing fancy. Just tried your mom’s pulao recipe from that diary.”

Rhea (tired):

“Vivaan, I’m not really—”

Vivaan (cutting softly):

“You haven’t eaten since morning. Sit. Please.”

She hesitated, then nodded quietly and joined him at the table.

The meal was simple, comforting. For a few minutes, they ate in silence—until Rhea finally set her spoon down and whispered:

Rhea:

“I went to see Dadu.”

Vivaan looked at her—not surprised, just present.

Rhea:

“He… praised the numbers. Said Papa would’ve been proud. Then—he said if I want the board to approve my CEO position and receive the shares… I can’t marry you.”

Vivaan’s hand paused mid-air, then lowered slowly. He didn’t look shocked. Just still.

Vivaan (after a beat):

“I figured something like that might come.”

Rhea:

“I didn’t say anything at first. But when he spoke about you… like you were some kind of mistake I was making—”

Her throat caught. She looked away.

“I tried to make him see. But he wouldn’t listen.”

Vivaan reached across the table and gently took her hand.

Vivaan (softly):

“You don’t have to make me your decision.”

She looked at him, searching his face.

Vivaan:

“If this is what your father wanted—if being CEO, owning what he built, fulfilling that legacy… if that’s what he dreamed for you—then go for it.”

Rhea:

“And lose you?”

Vivaan:

“You’re not losing me. You’re just… choosing something bigger than both of us right now.”

She looked down, blinking hard.

Vivaan (smiling faintly):

“Besides, I’m not going anywhere. I’ll still be here—whether you’re Rhea Mehra or Rhea Kapoor, CEO of a billion-dollar empire.”

She squeezed his hand back, but the silence between them said what they both feared: right now, it felt like she had no choice but to choose.

On the next day;

Dilip sat behind his grand mahogany desk, papers neatly arranged before him. Sunlight filtered in through the large window, casting long shadows on the polished floor. There was nothing casual about this moment. It was business, legacy, and bloodline—colliding.

Rhea stood tall, her eyes calm but worn. A quiet exhaustion rested behind them—the weight of mourning still fresh in her bones, but steadied by resolve.

Dilip (gently):

"You haven’t been sleeping well."

(pauses)

"I know it’s not easy. Your father… he would have wanted you to smile again. To remember him the right way, not stay buried in the loss."

Rhea (softly):

"I try. Every day I try."

Dilip didn’t say anything for a beat. He reached for a document and slid it forward.

Dilip:

"Your appointment as CEO. All formalities cleared. The board respects numbers more than names, and you’ve given them numbers they never imagined. Record-breaking profits in the hardest quarter we've ever seen."

(pauses, voice lower)

"I’m proud of you. You’ve made the Kapoor name proud."

Rhea’s hand hovered over the paper. She looked at it for a moment—really looked—then signed her name in a steady stroke. She pushed the file back to him.

Dilip nodded… and then reached for another document.

Dilip:

"This one’s personal. It says that you agree not to marry—at least not someone who doesn't match the standards we’ve built over generations."

Vivaan looked up slightly, but didn’t speak. Rhea didn’t look surprised. She already knew this was coming.

Dilip (firmly):

"Rhea, you’re Dilip Kapoor’s granddaughter. That means something. It always will. Vivaan may be a decent man, but he will never belong in this world—not truly. And that world will never forgive you for making a sentimental mistake."

Rhea finally met his eyes.

Rhea (calm, respectful):

"I know where I come from. I’ve carried the name with grace, with numbers, and with integrity. But I won’t reject love because it doesn’t wear a designer watch."

She took the second paper, looked at it for a breath, and signed.

Rhea:

"There. I’ve signed. I will not marry Vivaan. That satisfies the contract."

Dilip leaned back slightly. Just when he thought it was done, Rhea continued.

Rhea (voice steady):

"But I will not leave him either. He’s the man I love. You asked for a signature, and I gave it. But love isn’t something you notarize."

Vivaan stepped forward just a little. Not enough to provoke, but enough to stand beside her—not behind.

Vivaan (quietly):

"I’m not here for position. I’m here for her. Always have been."

Dilip stared at him—long and hard. But then something cracked, just slightly, behind his cold eyes.

Dilip (after a beat, voice softer):

"You’re a stubborn girl… just like your father."

(long pause)

"You’re really a Kapoor."

He stood up slowly. For the first time in years, there was no war left in his posture.

Dilip (gruffly):

"Go. Make your legacy. I’ve said what I had to."

Rhea nodded once. Vivaan gave a small bow—not forced, but respectful.

They turned and walked out of the room. As they reached the hallway, Dilip watched them go, standing alone in the quiet study.

And just before the door closed—

—he smiled.

Only for a second. But it was enough.

A flicker of something soft in the storm-hardened eyes of a man who had finally learned that strength wasn’t always about control.

It was about letting go.

Her father was gone. Her heart ached, even in triumph.

But maybe, just maybe, he’d be proud—

Not just because she fulfilled his dream,

But because she didn’t lose hers in the process.

As Dilip watched her leave, her hand in Vivaan’s,

Even the silence seemed to nod in approval.

Some legacies aren’t passed down.

They’re rewritten.
