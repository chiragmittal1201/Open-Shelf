Chapter 11

Out of Reach

The first sliver of light had begun to slip through the wooden blinds, casting long shadows across the bedroom floor.

The room was silent-save for the faint rustle of sheets and the distant sound of the city slowly waking up outside.

Vivaan stirred first.

His eyes blinked open slowly, adjusting to the soft morning light.

For a moment, he simply lay there, still tucked into the warmth of the previous night.

And then, as his gaze shifted to the figure beside him, his chest swelled with something quiet and immense.

Rhea.

She was lying on her side, facing him, a cream-colored blanket tangled around her lower half.

Her skin glowed faintly in the light-warm, soft, untouched by the rush of the world.

One delicate arm was tucked beneath her cheek, her hair spilling in disarray across the pillow and her bare shoulder.

Her lips were parted slightly, her breath even and peaceful.

She looked like a candy toy, fragile and tender-an impossible combination of innocence and desire.

Her face, relaxed in sleep, held no trace of the fierce woman who stood up to boardrooms or confronted ghosts from her past.

Here, she was just Rhea. His Rhea.

He smiled-genuine, wide, and boyish. And in that stillness, the night whispered back to him.

The way her hands had trembled on his skin. The way her breath had caught between kisses. The heat. The gasps. The softness of her curves beneath his fingers.

The surrender of it all.

A flush of warmth passed through him, not from desire, but from disbelief.

She had let him in-into her home, her arms, her heart.

Quietly, he slipped out of bed, careful not to wake her.

The wooden floor felt cool against his feet as he padded toward the kitchen, wearing nothing but his underwear.

As he moved, he glanced once over his shoulder-just to look at her again. He couldn't help it.

Rhea, meanwhile, slowly began to stir.

Her brows knit slightly in her sleep, and she turned her head lazily toward the other side of the bed, reaching out with half-awareness.

The sheets were empty.

Her eyes opened.

Confusion flickered across her face, brief but instinctive. The place where he had been-warm, but now vacant.

Where had he gone?

Then her gaze landed on the floor, on the rust-colored dress crumpled beside the bed, and her breath caught.

The night flooded back.

The taste of his mouth. The heat of his skin.

The way he had made her feel-like she was the only woman to exist.

Her body remembered even more than her mind did, the memories so vivid, they played like scenes.

His mouth against her collarbone. Her fingers in his hair.

The way he had whispered her name like a secret prayer.

She sat up slowly, holding the blanket against her chest. Her lips parted slightly, lost in the rush of memory.

Then she stood, wrapping the blanket around her, and stepped lightly out of the bedroom.

The scent of something warm-spiced and buttery-greeted her nose as she moved down the hallway.

And there he was.

Standing by the stove, barefoot, wearing nothing but his black boxer-briefs.

His back was to her, his hair slightly messy, and the muscles of his back flexed gently as he flipped something in the pan.

The morning sun poured through the windows, gilding the kitchen in a soft, amber glow-and him with it.

Rhea stood there for a moment, just watching.

Admiring.

He was, quite simply, breathtaking.

Then, without a word, she stepped forward and wrapped her arms around his waist from behind-her cheek resting against his back.

He paused, letting out the softest breath as her touch grounded him.

"Good morning," she whispered.

Vivaan didn't turn.

Rhea's arms circled around Vivaan's bare torso, the soft rustle of the blanket brushing against his skin.

She pressed her cheek to his back, the warmth of his body grounding her in the present.

He paused for a moment, smiling faintly as he stirred the eggs, letting the silence wrap around them like another blanket.

"Good morning," he said softly, not turning. His voice was deeper, still coated in sleep, and oddly comforting.

Rhea nuzzled into him, murmuring, "Smells like you're trying to bribe me into falling in love again."

Vivaan chuckled, finally turning his head slightly to catch a glimpse of her sleepy face. "And is it working?"

She pulled back just enough to look at him, the blanket slipping a little lower across her back, but she didn't seem to care.

"Almost unfairly well."

He plated the scrambled eggs and slid two slices of crisp toast onto a second plate. "Taste test?"

She reached forward and pinched a small bite right from the pan. He didn't stop her.

Her eyes lit up as she chewed. "Wow. This is... ridiculously good. I'm starting to wonder if you were a chef in a past life."

"Maybe," he grinned. "Or maybe it's just that I had the right motivation this morning."

She rolled her eyes playfully, then helped him carry the plates to the dining table.

Rhea settled into one of the chairs, adjusting the blanket around her like a loose robe.

Vivaan joined her, and for a few quiet minutes, they simply ate - content in the closeness, in the sounds of forks clinking and a city just beginning to stir outside.

Vivaan was the one who broke the silence. "Last night... felt like a beginning."

Rhea looked up from her toast, her expression softer than he'd ever seen it. "It did, didn't it?"

She held his gaze. "I've been scared of beginnings for a long time."

"I know," he said quietly. "But we're here now. And I'm not going anywhere."

She swallowed, hard. The words settled in her like an anchor and a lifeline, both.

"My dad used to say the same thing," she said, voice quieter now, almost distant.

"Whenever things got overwhelming - the board, school, even just life - he'd take me to the Maldives."

Vivaan blinked. "Maldives?"

Rhea nodded. "We have a private island there. Not the extravagant kind, not overly massive or flashy - just... peaceful. Simple in a way only privilege can afford, I guess."

She gave a small, self-aware smile. "But it felt like ours. The sea, the house, the quiet. That's where I felt closest to him."

Vivaan's tone was gentle. "Have you been back since...?"

She shook her head. "Not since his coma. I couldn't. It felt wrong without him. But now... after everything, I think I want to go. Not to escape. Just... to remember who I was before things got so complicated."

Vivaan reached across the table and placed his hand over hers. "Then let's go."

Her eyes lifted, searching him for something - permission, reassurance, hope. She found all three.

"You sure?" she asked, a small, almost scared smile tugging at her lips.

He nodded, thumb brushing her knuckles. "I'm sure."

And in the quiet that followed, it was clear: this wasn't just about a trip.

It was about finding peace with the past, with each other, and with themselves.

And then they were in the Maldives.

The sky was a pale canvas of gold and blue as the seaplane skimmed the gentle turquoise waters, its descent slow, elegant, and deliberate-much like everything that followed.

Vivaan stepped down from the small plane, barefoot onto warm wooden planks that stretched out like welcoming arms into the soft, white sand.

The breeze carried the scent of salt and frangipani. The island stretched before him-lush, quiet, and almost untouched, except for the soft silhouettes of a few staff at a distance, barely visible, trained to dissolve into the background.

Beside him, Rhea stood barefoot too, her linen shirt fluttering slightly in the breeze.

She looked not like a CEO, nor a lover-but something softer.

Like someone who had once belonged to this place and was just now returning home.

Vivaan took a moment. The stillness of it. The hum of the ocean.

The way the island seemed untouched by urgency. Then he looked at her.

"You said it was simple," he said, half-laughing, eyes tracing the sweeping villa that emerged from the palms.

It wasn't ostentatious-just elegant in a way that whispered wealth rather than shouted it.

Rhea smiled, brushing hair from her face. "It is," she said, like the word meant something deeper here.

"Come. Let me show you."

They walked barefoot across the soft sand, past low stone walls and curved garden paths rimmed with wild hibiscus.

The house appeared slowly-whitewashed, with open arches and high wooden beams. Its architecture breathed rather than stood still.

She opened the first door with a kind of reverence, fingers hesitating on the handle for a second too long.

"This..." she said, stepping inside, "is where Dad taught me to play chess."

The room was sun-dappled, a large wooden table sitting in the center, old books piled in corners.

Vivaan followed her quietly, eyes tracing everything.

"And here," she pointed through an archway leading to a side terrace, "this is where we used to watch meteor showers every summer. We'd bring blankets and lie on the cold stone and try to count the streaks."

Vivaan didn't speak. He didn't want to. He was too busy observing-Rhea, and the way her voice curled slightly when she remembered, and the way her fingers skimmed the surfaces like they still carried echoes.

And in that moment, she didn't look like the woman who'd faced boardrooms or press interviews or buried grief.

She looked like a little girl, full of stories.

His heart softened in a way he hadn't expected.

He trailed his fingers along the wooden frame of a window as if trying to absorb the life that had once bloomed here.

"I wish I'd known you then," he murmured.

She glanced at him, a quiet smile curving on her lips.

"You'd have hated me. I was bossy. Told everyone how to build their sandcastles."

"I think I'd have let you boss me around," he said gently, and she laughed-light and unburdened.

In one of the rooms, half library and half memory box, she pulled out a book from a shelf and flipped to the back.

A photo slipped out-crinkled at the edges. A younger Rhea, wild curls and toothy grin, sitting on her father's shoulders.

Vivaan took it. Held it delicately.

"She loved hard, even back then," he whispered, brushing a thumb over the image.

Then he looked up, and without another word, kissed her forehead-soft and sure.

Outside, the wind rustled the palms, and the ocean breathed against the shore.

Inside, a man and a woman stood in a house that once belonged to a father and a daughter.

And somehow, it all felt whole again.

Rhea's fingers lingered on the frame of an old photo-her, barefoot and sun-kissed, sitting on her father's shoulders, both laughing into the wind.

"I used to think this place had a heartbeat," she said softly.

"Like it only existed when we were here together."

Vivaan didn't say anything. He just stepped closer and wrapped his arms around her from behind, holding her still.

She leaned into him, letting the quiet speak. Her eyes shimmered, but no tears fell.

"It doesn't feel lonely this time," she whispered. "That's new."

Vivaan kissed the side of her head gently, resting his chin on her shoulder.

"Maybe it's still beating," he said. "Just differently now."

They stood like that for a long moment-surrounded by memories, but no longer consumed by them.

And somehow, it all felt whole again.

Next day, Vivaan stood near the shaded cabana, talking quietly to one of the staff members.

His voice was low but certain. "Set everything up just the way I asked. I'll explain the rest later."

From the corner of his eye, movement caught his attention-light and fluid. He turned.

Rhea was emerging from the ocean, droplets cascading down her skin, her swimsuit clinging like sunlight on water.

Her hair, wet and tousled, framed her face like a halo.

She pushed it back with a soft, careless motion and smiled, unaware of how the moment captured him entirely.

Vivaan's gaze softened. How did I get someone so beautiful, so joyful, so entirely... mine?

The thought made his heart ache with a strange protectiveness. I'll protect her. No matter what.

She jogged toward him, salt and laughter in her steps, and without warning, grabbed his wrist.

"Enough talking to staff, Mr. Serious. Come on!"

"I-hey, I wasn't done-" he stammered, turning back toward the staff.

"You can brief them later!" she laughed, tugging harder.

They ran together toward the water, and the ocean greeted them with warmth.

Splashing, spinning, laughter-Vivaan caught her from behind and spun her in wide circles, her head thrown back in delight, hands gripping his shoulders as water arced around them.

He set her down, breathless. They stood close, chests rising with laughter.

"Underwater time?" she grinned, nudging him.

Vivaan hesitated. "I've never done this."

"You'll love it."

"I don't know..."

"Well then," she said, playfully dramatic, "I'm going alone. Hope the sharks don't eat me."

"There are sharks!?"

"Who knows?" She turned, taking a teasing step toward the gear.

"Wait-hell no, you're not going alone!" Vivaan blurted. "I'm coming!"

Rhea smirked over her shoulder. "Knew you would."

Moments later, they descended together, helmets in place, into the quiet magic of the reef.

Colors exploded around them-schools of vibrant fish darting like rainbows made of silk. Coral bloomed like underwater gardens.

Everything moved in slow motion, like a dream you don't want to wake from.

Vivaan's hand found Rhea's. Her eyes were wide, childlike, full of wonder.

As they moved deeper into the coral cradle, the world blurred into color and quiet.

Rhea drifted ahead, her body graceful, unhurried. A ripple of neon blue fish curved around her like paint swirls in water.

Then something shifted.

She tilted her head, reached her hand out-not just to observe, but to engage.

A dozen tiny fish hovered in front of her, pulsing like little hearts in the sea.

Rhea cupped her hands gently, as if asking them to trust her. One darted forward, brushed her fingertips.

She didn't flinch-she beamed.

And then she started... playing.

She swirled her fingers in slow circles, guiding the fish like she was conducting an invisible orchestra.

She wasn't chasing them-she was inviting them. And somehow, they listened.

Vivaan froze mid-kick, his breath catching.

You're more than a woman I love, he thought. You're a whole world I never knew I needed.

She twirled in the water, hair floating like ink in silence, laughing soundlessly behind the helmet.

The fish twirled with her, and in that moment, she didn't look like someone trying to escape the world.

She looked like someone the world had paused for.

Vivaan felt a rush of something bigger than awe. He wasn't just in love with her-he was in love with her essence.

The way she existed with wonder still intact. The way she could create magic without trying.

You're joy, he realized. Unfiltered. Untamed. Unteachable.

And without thinking, he reached for her hand again-because some moments don't need to be shared.

They just need to be held.

In the Evening, The sun was beginning its slow descent, melting into the horizon in warm streaks of amber and coral.

Waves whispered secrets as they kissed the edges of the white sand, and in the heart of the private island, Vivaan stood barefoot on a wooden deck built right above the shoreline-where ocean met dreams.

The setting was intimate, intentional. A wide, inviting bed was placed on the polished deck, layered in crisp white linen, surrounded by sheer curtains that fluttered like breath in the breeze.

Tiny glass lanterns hung from curved hooks, each carrying a soft candlelight that would glow into the night.

The bed wasn't just for rest-it was an unspoken promise, a heartbeat echoing against the tide.

Just a few feet away, nestled into the sand, was a dinner table-round, draped in sheer ivory, with seashell candle holders glowing like captured stardust.

Two wine glasses sparkled in anticipation, and the cutlery was set with an artistry that felt more ceremonial than casual.

Nearby, small lanterns floated inside glass domes, casting a warm halo across the scene.

The ocean, the stars, and the breeze all felt invited.

Vivaan stood quietly, adjusting a flower in one of the centerpieces, his eyes scanning the setup like a painter adding finishing touches.

Everything had to be perfect-not luxurious, but beautiful. Not overwhelming, but unforgettable.

I hope she likes the surprise... he thought, turning his eyes toward the path that led from the house.

Where is she...?

And then-

She appeared.

He looked up. And froze.

Rhea was walking across the sand toward him, barefoot, her hair loose and flowing like the night breeze itself.

The white net kimono she wore swayed like liquid light, untied, open at the front to reveal soft glimpses of the white brassiere and matching bottoms beneath-like innocence wrapped in daring, elegance dipped in sensuality.

Her skin glowed golden under the moon, her body catching the starlight like she belonged more to the sky than the earth.

She waved playfully at him.

Vivaan blinked.

Yes, he was stunned. How could he not be?

"She looked like a vanilla cloud kissed by sun," he thought. "Soft, light, and utterly out of reach-until now."

Rhea stopped a few feet from him. "Why are you looking at me like that?"

He didn't answer.

Vivaan took a step toward her... then paused. His eyes caught the small flower vase on the dinner table.

A single white bloom peeked out, innocent and perfect.

He plucked it.

And then, he dropped to one knee.

Rhea's expression broke into surprise, her steps halting as the waves murmured behind them. "Wait... what are you doing?"

she asked, a grin teasing her lips, though her voice trembled slightly.

Vivaan looked up at her, holding the flower out-not like it was just a bloom, but like it was a vow.

"I didn't plan this," he said, softly. "This wasn't part of the evening. But the moment I saw you walking toward me like that... I knew I couldn't just let it pass."

She blinked.

"I love you, Rhea."

His voice cracked slightly-real, raw.

"I don't have a ring. I don't have answers. But I'm ready to fight for you if I have to. Just... say yes. Be mine."

There was a moment of stillness. Just the ocean. Just them.

Then Rhea, with a small laugh-more wonder than amusement-stepped forward, bent gently, and touched his cheek.

"You didn't plan this..." she echoed.

"No," he admitted, smiling sheepishly.

"And yet here we are." She took the flower from his hand and tucked it behind her ear, leaning down to kiss his forehead.

"I love you too, Vivaan. And yes... I'm yours."

His arms wrapped around her waist as he stood. Their bodies met in a soft, close hug as the ocean roared like applause in the background.

And with that-under the bruised-gold sky, barefoot on sand, no ring but hearts full-they made a promise far louder than vows.

The sky was beginning to blush into twilight, casting long, gentle shadows across the wooden deck as the waves whispered close to the shore.

A soft breeze danced through the air, carrying the scent of salt and something faintly floral-maybe the candles, or maybe just the moment.

Rhea and Vivaan had moved from the bed to the beautifully arranged table nearby.

Two seats, candlelight flickering between them, a soft white tablecloth rippling with the sea breeze, and dishes that looked too perfect to touch.

But they didn't care for the food-not really.

Vivaan, still slightly breathless from the way Rhea had accepted his sudden proposal, pulled her chair out with an exaggerated gentlemanly flourish.

"Madam," he grinned.

Rhea sat down slowly, eyes not leaving his for a second.

"So much chivalry tonight. Are you planning to seduce me with manners?"

He leaned down, letting his lips graze just barely against the shell of her ear. "Only if they work."

Rhea gave a small, delicious shiver. "They just might," she murmured, reaching up and letting her fingers trace slowly down the side of his neck as he sat beside her.

Her nails dragged gently through the open collar of his shirt, just enough to make his skin tense.

Dinner was served quietly by the distant helpers, who disappeared as quickly as they came, leaving them alone under the open sky.

Vivaan offered her a bite with his fork, intentionally missing her lips and grazing the edge of her lower lip instead.

"Oops," he said, fake-innocent.

"Oh, you're terrible," Rhea laughed, but she leaned closer anyway, stealing a bite from his plate instead.

Her thigh brushed against his under the table-lingering, not accidental-and she didn't pull away.

"You're in trouble tonight," she whispered, mischief in her eyes.

Vivaan smirked. "Then I hope I never get out of it."

Her hand slid slowly to his knee under the table, fingers tapping lightly as if counting his heartbeat.

"This is dangerous."

"Us?"

"This whole night," she said, her voice soft, but heavy with heat.

"The moon. This setup. You in that shirt. Me in..." She trailed off and gestured vaguely at her own body.

"All this."

"You knew what you were doing," Vivaan said, his voice low now, eyes never leaving hers.

"Coming to me looking like a wish someone whispered to the sea."

Rhea blushed but didn't back down. "And you... you're everything I'd want to get lost with."

They sat close now, not even pretending to eat anymore.

Her foot found his under the table and slowly slid up his leg, playful, slow, challenging.

His fingers wrapped around hers across the table-gently at first, then with a slight pull that told her exactly what he was thinking.

But they didn't rush.

Instead, Rhea took a strawberry from a small silver bowl, held it up, and said, "You want it?"

Vivaan nodded slowly.

She brought it to his lips, only to take a bite herself at the last moment. Juice stained her lips.

Vivaan leaned forward, brushed it off with his thumb-then sucked it from his own finger.

Rhea's eyes darkened.

They sat there, half-laughing, half-burning. And all around them, the ocean moved like a lullaby, the stars began to wake, and the night-quietly, surely-wrapped itself around the two lovers who knew exactly where this was going... but had no idea what waited just beyond the tide.

It was almost impossible to finish dinner after the way they'd touched and teased each other, but somehow, they managed-though neither remembered how the food tasted.

Their appetite had shifted elsewhere.

Rhea leaned back in her chair, stretching her toes into the cool sand, a little dreamy smile on her lips.

Vivaan stood, brushing invisible crumbs from his shirt, and offered a hand.

She looked up, tilting her head. "What now?"

He didn't reply. Just stepped close, wrapped an arm around her waist, and the other beneath her knees.

She let out a surprised gasp as he lifted her up in one swoop.

"Vivaan!" she half-laughed, arms flung around his neck. "You're not serious-"

But he was.

He carried her across the moonlit deck, the sound of the waves blending with the soft rustle of her kimono.

She felt like a feather in his arms, weightless in more ways than one.

The bed greeted them like a dream-soft, low, luxurious.

Vivaan leaned forward and gently dropped her onto it.

She bounced slightly on the sheets, giggling.

He stepped close again.

With a grin playing on his lips, he reached for the knot at her waist and tugged.

The sheer white fabric fell apart like a sigh, revealing soft skin, delicate lace, and a heart already racing.

Rhea narrowed her eyes with a smirk. "You're getting bold, Mr. Mehra."

Vivaan leaned down, his voice low.

"Bold?" He chuckled. "You haven't even seen my reckless side yet."

She made a mock-pout, puckering her lips like a child demanding a kiss. "Then what's stopping you?" she teased.

"Don't tell me the great Vivaan Mehra has stage fright?"

He brushed a finger along her collarbone.

"I just like watching you beg."

She gasped, scandalized in the most delicious way. "Excuse me?! I don't beg."

He leaned closer, their noses almost touching.

"No?" he whispered. "Not even a little...?"

She grabbed a pillow and hit him lightly.

"You're such a tease!"

He laughed, then dipped down again-lips hovering above hers, breath mingling.

"Rhea..." he whispered. "Do you have any idea what you do to me?"

Her fingers slipped under the open part of his shirt, feeling the warm ridges of his chest.

"I'm hoping it's at least half of what you're doing to me," she murmured, almost out of breath now.

He leaned in like he was finally-finally-going to kiss her.

But instead, he pulled back again, running his lips down her cheek without touching her mouth.

She whined, frustrated. "You're killing me."

"Sweet deaths," he said with a grin, "are worth dying for."

Then, suddenly, she grabbed his shirt and yanked him down.

"Enough poetry," she said. "Just kiss me, Mehra."

And in that charged, electric moment-when their lips were barely a breath away-

A sudden presence made them freeze.

They both turned. A member of the staff had approached quietly from the side of the deck, unsure of where to look.

His expression was awkward, but serious.

He held a satellite phone with both hands, gaze respectfully averted, clearly trying not to notice how barely dressed Rhea was or how intimate the moment had been.

Rhea instantly sat up, trying to gather her robe around her, breath still caught in her throat.

Her face was a mix of flushed embarrassment and immediate tension.

No words were exchanged. There was no need.

The staff member extended the phone. That was enough.

Rhea stared at it, then slowly reached out and took it.

Her fingers trembled slightly.

Vivaan stood a step behind her, watching, shirt still undone, his entire mood flipped like a switch.

She answered.

A few seconds of silence.

And then, her face changed.

The color drained. Her lips parted, eyes wide. A single tear escaped the corner of her eye.

She didn't say a word.

She just stared ahead, motionless, phone still pressed to her ear.

Vivaan saw her shoulders slump.

Saw the way her body folded inward, like she was trying to hold something inside.

He stepped forward, slowly. "Rhea...?"

But she didn't look at him.

The only answer he got... was the silence between waves.
