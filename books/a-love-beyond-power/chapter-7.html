Chapter 7

The Unspoken Understanding

Vivaan stepped into Kapoor Fashion’s headquarters with a quiet determination in his stride. He wasn’t second-guessing anymore. Today, he had made up his mind.

As he passed through the corridors, his eyes scanned the familiar faces—employees bustling around, the low murmur of conversations, the weight of the company’s revival resting in the air. But none of it distracted him.

He had something to do.

Spotting Rajat, one of the senior PR strategists from his secret team, Vivaan walked straight to him.

“Is everything ready?” he asked in a firm tone.

Rajat nodded but hesitated for a moment. “Yeah, we set everything up just like you wanted. But Vivaan… are you sure about this?”

Vivaan narrowed his eyes slightly. “Why wouldn’t I be?”

Rajat exhaled, rubbing the back of his neck. “Because Rhea is working on the exact same thing. She’s planning the execution for the raw look campaign as we speak. What if she already has a plan?”

A smirk tugged at the corner of Vivaan’s lips. “She does. And she doesn’t even realize she already approved mine.”

Rajat stared at him, confused. But Vivaan didn’t elaborate. Instead, he clapped Rajat’s shoulder and walked away.

It was time.

Rhea sat in her cabin, flipping through a few campaign notes, her mind racing through the execution steps.

The meeting earlier had gone well, and now, she needed to finalize the execution strategy. The weight of Kapoor Fashion’s revival sat on her shoulders, but for the first time, she wasn’t doubting herself.

She was about to make some adjustments when—

Vivaan walked in without knocking.

She barely looked up. “Do you have a habit of barging in unannounced?”

Vivaan leaned against the doorframe, arms crossed. “Come with me.”

Rhea finally glanced up, her brow furrowing. “What?”

“Just come with me. I need to show you something.”

Rhea put down her notes, sighing. “Vivaan, I’m in the middle of something. If you have something to say, say it here.”

Instead of answering, Vivaan walked up to her chair, grabbed her wrist gently, and pulled her up.

“Vivaan!” she protested, stumbling slightly as she was forced to her feet.

“You ask too many questions, Rhea. Just follow me.”

And before she could argue, he was already leading her out of the cabin.

The heels of Rhea’s shoes clicked against the floor as she half-walked, half-stumbled behind Vivaan.

“Vivaan, where are you taking me?” she asked again, trying to keep up.

“You’ll see.”

“This is ridiculous—”

She tried to pull back, but Vivaan tightened his grip just slightly, not enough to hurt, but enough to remind her he wasn’t letting go.

They rounded a corner when suddenly, a staff member carrying a pile of files walked straight into their path.

Vivaan instinctively pulled Rhea closer, his hand moving to her waist as he twisted slightly to shield her.

The movement was natural, but the effect wasn’t.

For a fraction of a second, Rhea found herself inches away from him—so close she could feel the warmth of his body against hers.

Their eyes met.

Something unspoken flickered between them.

Rhea’s breath hitched.

Vivaan didn’t move either. His fingers still rested lightly on her waist, his grip on her wrist still warm.

Neither of them spoke.

And then, as if realizing the moment had stretched too long, Vivaan suddenly let go.

“You really need to stop resisting me, Rhea,” he muttered, his voice lower than usual.

Rhea blinked, her heart skipping an unfamiliar beat. “I—what?”

He smirked slightly. “Relax. I’m not kidnapping you.”

Still recovering from the sudden proximity, Rhea straightened herself and cleared her throat.

“Could’ve fooled me.”

Vivaan chuckled, shaking his head. “Come on. We’re almost there.”

And before Rhea could regain full control of her thoughts, he was leading her forward once again.

But this time, her steps weren’t as resistant as before.

Vivaan pushed open the door to the presentation room, guiding Rhea inside before letting go of her wrist.

Inside, a small group of PR and marketing team members stood around a large screen, their eyes filled with anticipation.

Rhea’s gaze flickered between them and Vivaan. “Okay, what is this?”

Instead of answering, Vivaan walked over to the panel, tapped a few keys, and the screen lit up—revealing a detailed presentation of the raw look campaign execution plan.

Rhea’s breath caught for a second.

She knew this plan.

No—this was her plan she was planning.

Every step, every rollout strategy—it was exactly what she had just been planning in her cabin.

She turned to Vivaan, eyes narrowing. "What is this?"

Vivaan leaned against the table casually. "The execution plan for Kapoor Fashion’s revival."

Her brows furrowed deeper. "This is my plan."

Vivaan tilted his head. "Is it?"

She turned back to the screen, scanning the details. A sense of unease crept over her.

Every single step matched what she had envisioned. The only difference?

Vivaan had finished it before she even started.

Her stomach dropped.

She hadn’t approved his plan directly.

But she did approve it—because it was exactly what she had envisioned.

Vivaan had been ahead of her the whole time.

Her gaze flickered across the room—to the team members, standing tall and confident. They weren’t waiting for her critique.

They were waiting for her approval.

And then—it hit her.

This wasn’t something Vivaan had pulled together last week.

The timelines, the preparations… he had been working with his team on this for more than four months.

She lifted her gaze to Vivaan realizing his efforts of four months, something unsettlingly intense settling in her chest.

Four months.

For four months, he had been planning for a company that wasn’t even his.

For a second, she didn’t know what to say.

Vivaan straightened, breaking the silence. “So? Are you going to yell at me, or admit I saved you weeks of planning?”

Rhea exhaled sharply, running a hand through her hair. “I should yell at you.”

Vivaan tilted his head. “But you won’t.”

She shook her head, a reluctant smile tugging at her lips. “No. I won’t.”

Rhea exhaled sharply, schooling her expression into something more composed. She turned to the team.

“This is impressive work,” she said, her voice carrying the weight of leadership. “Team your dedication to this campaign doesn’t go unnoticed. Kapoor Fashion needs this, and I appreciate the effort you’ve put in.”

She didn’t look at Vivaan.

Didn’t need to.

Because somehow, this felt right.

And that realization unsettled her more than anything else.

(After Some days)

Rhea placed a file on the desk and glanced at Vivaan, who was still glued to his screen. His desk was empty—no lunchbox, no food in sight.

"You forgot your lunch again, didn’t you?" she asked, crossing her arms.

Vivaan looked up, slightly surprised. "Huh? Oh… yeah, I guess I did."

Rhea sighed, shaking her head. "This is the third time this week, Vivaan. Are you trying to survive on coffee?"

He grinned. "Caffeine is a legitimate food group."

"Not when you’re running a company. Come on, we’re eating outside," she said, grabbing her bag.

He blinked. "Wait, what? Now?"

"Yes. Now." Her voice held quiet authority, leaving no room for debate.

Minutes later, they were seated at a quiet restaurant near the office. Rhea pushed a plate toward him. "Eat."

Vivaan smirked. "You’re bossy even outside work."

"And you’re reckless with your health," she shot back, stirring her coffee. "You didn’t use to be like this."

Vivaan raised an eyebrow. "Oh? And what was I like before?"

Rhea paused, considering. "More laid-back. More… yourself."

Vivaan shrugged. "Things changed."

She studied him, feeling something shift in the conversation. "You were always the one reminding me to eat when I got too caught up in work. Now, look at us."

He chuckled. "Guess we switched roles."

The ease between them made the air feel warmer, more personal. But just as Rhea was about to steer the conversation elsewhere, Vivaan suddenly said, "Do you regret it?"

She frowned. "Regret what?"

"Leaving. Everything that happened after."

Her fingers tensed around the cup, but her voice remained steady. "I didn’t have a choice, Vivaan."

"You did," he said quietly. "But you didn’t trust anyone enough to stay."

The words landed harder than he probably intended. She didn’t respond immediately, instead looking at the steam rising from her coffee.

Flashback (8 months ago):

They sat across from each other in the Kapoor Fashion cafeteria. Rhea, lost in back-to-back meetings, barely noticed the untouched plate in front of her.

"Rhea, eat," Vivaan nudged her tray toward her. "Skipping meals won’t make you more productive."

She had laughed back then. "And since when did you become my personal health coach?"

"Since you started acting like a robot who forgets basic human needs."

Back to Present

Rhea exhaled, pushing her plate aside. "I should’ve handled things differently. But back then… I felt like I had no control."

Vivaan leaned back, watching her. "And now?"

She looked up, holding his gaze. "Now… I take control."

There was something in the way she said it—something final, something unshakable.

Vivaan gave a small smile. "Good. Because Kapoor Fashion needs you."

Rhea tilted her head, a teasing glint in her eyes. "And you?"

His fingers stilled on his glass for half a second before he smirked. "I just need my lunch on time."

She rolled her eyes, but the warmth between them lingered, unspoken.

As they stood to leave, Vivaan reached to take Rhea’s empty cup, but at the same moment, she moved her hand toward it. Their fingers brushed—not just a fleeting touch, but a slow, lingering moment.

Rhea felt something jolt through her. She expected Vivaan to pull away. But he didn’t.

For a second too long, they stayed like that—hands barely apart, a breath away from something neither of them understood yet.

Then, Vivaan cleared his throat and stepped back. "We should get back."

Rhea nodded, gripping her bag a little too tightly.

Neither of them acknowledged the moment.

But neither of them forgot it, either.

Back at the office, the air was different—not just between Rhea and Vivaan, but in the entire workspace. The employees moved with a renewed energy, as if Rhea’s return had breathed life into the company again.

Rhea walked ahead, flipping through some notes while Vivaan followed, his hands in his pockets.

"We’re heading to the studio," Vivaan said, casually keeping pace with her.

She stopped, glancing at him. "Studio?"

Vivaan nodded. "The designing team is setting up a test shoot for the campaign. Thought you’d want to be there."

Rhea considered it for a second before nodding. "Good call. I want to see the execution firsthand."

Vivaan smirked. "You mean you don’t trust me to handle it alone?"

Rhea didn’t bother looking up from her notes. "Not at all."

Vivaan chuckled. "Ah, there it is. The famous Rhea Kapoor trust issues."

She sighed dramatically. "If you want, I can let you handle everything."

Vivaan scoffed. "Yeah, and then watch you redo the entire thing because it’s ‘not up to standard’?"

She gave him a sideways glance. "See? You do know me well."

"Terrifyingly well," he muttered, shaking his head.

The studio was buzzing with activity. Designers adjusted outfits, photographers tested lighting, and models prepared for their shots.

Rhea observed everything carefully. "Looks good so far."

Vivaan stood beside her, hands in his pockets, watching her scan the set with sharp precision. "You look like you belong here."

She gave him a sideways glance. "Obviously. I’ve been in studios longer than you’ve been in this industry."

Vivaan smirked. "That’s true. You weren’t just running the company. You were the face of it."

Rhea let out a short breath, her expression unreadable. "A long time ago."

Vivaan tilted his head toward the cameras. "Doesn’t feel that long, though. You still have that presence."

Rhea shook her head. "I was never a model, Vivaan. It was just part of the job."

Vivaan's smirk deepened. "Maybe. But you knew how to own the frame. I remember watching you during those shoots—you never needed retakes."

She scoffed lightly. "It’s called efficiency. Not a hidden passion."

Vivaan chuckled. "Yeah? So when I took those test shots of you, and you looked straight into the lens like you owned it… that was just efficiency?"

She narrowed her eyes. "You’re really bringing that up now?"

Vivaan shrugged. "Just saying—it’s interesting how some people claim to hate something but are really, really good at it."

Rhea shook her head, exasperated. "Drop it, Vivaan."

He grinned but didn't push further. "Fine. But you do know I still have those pictures, right?"

Rhea stilled.

Vivaan caught the flicker of surprise on her face before she masked it.

He leaned slightly closer, lowering his voice just enough. "And they’re still my best shots."

Something about the way he said it—low, almost absentmindedly—made the air between them shift.

Rhea felt it immediately.

Vivaan did too.

For a moment, neither of them spoke.

Then, the head stylist called out, breaking the silence.

Rhea cleared her throat. "I’ll go check on the styling team."

Vivaan exhaled, rubbing his neck. "Yeah… good idea."

She walked away, but the weight of his words stayed with her.

In the evening Vivaan shut his laptop and stretched. "Okay, we’re almost done. I’ll fix the last few things tomorrow—"

"Come home with me," Rhea said, cutting him off.

Vivaan blinked. "Excuse me?"

"We still have pending work, and I don’t want to waste time tomorrow."

Vivaan exhaled. "Rhea, it's late, and I have a—"

She raised an eyebrow. "A what?"

Vivaan hesitated. "A life?"

Rhea gave him a flat look. "And you’re choosing now to live it?"

Vivaan sighed. "I just think we should finish this in the morning."

"Or, you could stop arguing and come now. Look at it this way—you’ll get to finish work, eat a proper meal, and avoid waking up early."

"That’s your sales pitch?" Vivaan smirked.

Rhea crossed her arms. "Are you coming or do I have to make it an official order?"

Vivaan exhaled dramatically. "You really don’t take no for an answer, do you?"

"Not when I know I’m right," she said, grabbing her bag. "Let’s go."

Vivaan shook his head, amused, but followed her.

Vivaan had been inside Rhea’s house before, but never like this—never as part of her world in a way that felt natural.

Instead of taking him to the living room, she led him straight to her library-turned-workspace, a space that was an extension of her mind—structured yet warm, elegant yet personal. The walls were lined with books, some fashion-related, others spanning art, business, and history. A sleek wooden desk sat in the center, papers stacked neatly on the side.

"You get settled. I’ll be back in five minutes," Rhea said, disappearing into her bedroom.

Vivaan set up his laptop, but when she returned, his focus completely derailed.

She had changed into a soft, oversized sweatshirt that slipped slightly off one shoulder and fitted joggers that hugged her just enough to make him notice. Her hair, usually styled for the office, was tied up in a loose bun, with a few strands falling against her face. She looked... effortlessly beautiful.

Vivaan wasn’t the kind of man who stared. But right now, he couldn't help it.

There was something about seeing her like this—not as the CEO, not as the guarded, sharp-tongued woman—but just as Rhea.

His gaze lingered for a second too long before he forced himself to look away.

Rhea noticed.

She didn't say anything, but the moment stretched between them, something unsaid passing in the silence.

"Alright, let’s get this done," she said, sitting beside him, completely unaware of the effect she had on him.

Vivaan cleared his throat. "Yeah… let’s."

They worked in sync. Rhea mapped out the campaign execution, and Vivaan adjusted visual strategies. Without needing long discussions, they instinctively understood each other’s direction.

A little while later, a helper entered, setting down a tray with food. "Ma’am, your dinner."

Rhea didn’t even glance up. "Just leave it here."

Vivaan looked at the tray and smirked. "You actually eat proper meals?"

Rhea gave him a look. "Shocking, I know." She pushed a plate toward him. "You should eat too."

"I should probably leave after this."

Rhea frowned. "You’ve been working all day. You barely eat at the office. Do I need to force you?"

Vivaan sighed. "Are you always this bossy outside work too?"

"Eat and find out," Rhea smirked.

Vivaan chuckled but picked up his fork.

They ate in easy silence, the quiet hum of the house surrounding them.

As they ate, Vivaan glanced at her. "You know… I don’t think I’ve ever heard you talk about your family. Except… well, your grandpa."

Rhea paused. "That’s because there isn’t much to talk about."

Vivaan didn’t push, but he didn’t drop it either.

After a moment, she sighed. "My dad was different. He wasn’t like my grandpa."

Vivaan stayed quiet, letting her speak at her own pace.

"He always made me feel… enough," Rhea said softly. "Like I didn’t have to prove anything to anyone. He believed in me, even before I believed in myself."

She ran a finger along the edge of her plate. "Even when I made mistakes, he never made me feel like I was failing. He just reminded me that I was learning."

Vivaan watched her. She wasn’t just talking about her father. She was remembering what it felt like to be truly supported.

"He’s still here," she continued, voice quieter. "But at the same time, he isn’t. And that’s the worst part. He’s lying in a hospital bed, and I don’t even know if he can hear me anymore."

Vivaan’s grip tightened around his fork. "Rhea…"

She exhaled sharply, shaking her head as if brushing the emotions away. "Anyway. That’s that."

Vivaan didn’t know what to say—because what do you say to something like that?

So he simply said, "He would be proud of you."

She looked at him. "You think so?"

"I know so."

For a moment, she held his gaze.

Then, she blinked and straightened. "Alright, back to work."

Vivaan nodded. "Back to business, boss."

A ghost of a smile played on her lips.

An hour later, they wrapped up the work. Rhea stretched, glancing at the clock. "It’s getting late. You should go."

Vivaan nodded, closing his laptop, feeling the lingering exhaustion from a long day. “Yeah, I should. Thanks for letting me stay.”

He pushed back from the desk, and as he stood, Rhea did the same. They both moved toward the door, but as Vivaan reached it, something about the space between them shifted.

It wasn’t planned. It wasn’t intentional. But as Vivaan stood near the door, Rhea moved just a bit closer than usual to walk him out. And in that slight shift, they found themselves standing close enough for Vivaan to feel the heat of her presence.

He froze.

There was something about the way she stood—poised, natural, her eyes meeting his with a quiet openness that made it impossible to ignore. His heart skipped.

For a split second, everything around them went still.

Vivaan felt an urge to reach out—just a touch. He could have stepped back, moved past the moment. But instead, his eyes lingered on her—the faintest trace of hesitation, then a thought he shouldn’t have entertained at all.

And then, before he could stop himself, Rhea’s hand brushed his wrist. Not like before—this time it was soft, almost lingering. A small, gentle gesture. Her fingers traced lightly over his skin, as if she hadn't even realized it herself.

But Vivaan did. And that touch, though fleeting, sizzled.

His breath hitched.

This was dangerous. Too dangerous.

Vivaan knew in an instant that this couldn’t go on. He couldn’t be this close. He was engaged to Sanya. His commitment was elsewhere, and this, whatever this was between him and Rhea, was crossing a line he shouldn’t even be standing near.

With one final, guilty glance at Rhea—whose fingers were still slightly curled, almost as if she was waiting for something—Vivaan quickly stepped back.

"I should go," he said, his voice thick with something unspoken.

Rhea nodded without a word. She didn’t seem to notice the weight of the moment—maybe she did, maybe she didn’t.

Vivaan didn’t wait for another second. He turned and walked out abruptly, his pulse hammering in his ears.

As he exited the building, he couldn’t shake the feeling of her touch lingering on his skin. That wasn’t good.

He had no business getting this close to her. It had been a slip—something they both could easily forget. But Vivaan knew better.

The lines were being blurred, and that terrified him.

By the time he reached his car, he was already thinking, this can’t happen again.

He was engaged to Sanya.

He had a life that was already set in motion.

But the questions began, too—why had it felt so easy to get lost in that moment? Why had he almost let it happen?

Vivaan started the engine, his hands gripping the wheel tightly, as if the movement would ground him, bring him back to reality. But as he drove off into the night, he couldn’t stop thinking about Rhea.

After some days, Dilip stood outside the glass-walled conference room, his hands clasped behind his back, watching. Inside, Rhea sat at the head of the table, completely engrossed in a discussion with the team. Papers were spread out before her, a tablet in her hand as she made notes with sharp precision.

She didn’t see him. She didn’t know he was there.

His eyes narrowed slightly, watching the way she commanded the room. There was no hesitation in her tone, no flicker of uncertainty in her posture. For the first time, he saw not just his granddaughter, but a leader.

Dilip exhaled sharply through his nose, turning away. He wouldn’t say it—not yet. But maybe, just maybe… she was proving him wrong.

In the Afternoon,

Vivaan’s cabin was unusually busy that afternoon. Rhea had practically taken over his desk, her laptop open, files scattered everywhere. Vivaan leaned against the edge of the desk, casually flipping through a document while they went over the campaign strategies.

“Are you always this bossy, or am I just special?” Vivaan teased, glancing at her.

Rhea smirked, not looking up. “Oh, you’re special, alright. Specially slow at reading those reports.”

Before Vivaan could respond, the cabin door swung open.

“Sanya?” Vivaan’s brows lifted in surprise.

Sanya stepped inside, wearing a deep emerald-green saree that hugged her curves gracefully. A delicate diamond necklace rested on her collarbone, her long hair perfectly styled in soft waves. She looked effortlessly elegant—exactly the kind of woman who belonged in Vivaan’s life.

She smiled, holding up a neatly packed lunchbox. “I brought you lunch! I figured you’d be too busy to eat.”

Vivaan blinked. “You didn’t have to—”

“Of course I had to.” Sanya’s voice was light, but there was a firmness to it. Her gaze shifted to Rhea, her smile polite but slightly curious.

Vivaan, realizing the gap in introductions, cleared his throat. “Uh, Sanya, this is Rhea. Rhea, my fiancée, Sanya.”

Rhea nodded, her expression unreadable. “Nice to meet you.”

Sanya smiled. “Same here. I’ve heard a lot about you.”

Rhea’s lips quirked up slightly. “All good things, I hope.”

Vivaan interjected before Sanya could respond, turning to Rhea. “Come on, have lunch with us.”

Sanya nodded in agreement. “Yes, join us! There’s plenty.”

Rhea waved a hand dismissively. “Oh no, I wouldn’t want to intrude. You two enjoy your romantic meal.” Her tone was light, teasing—but there was something beneath it, something even she didn’t quite understand.

Vivaan gave her a look, but Rhea was already gathering her files, preparing to continue working.

As he settled on the couch with Sanya, Rhea’s eyes flickered toward them. Sanya was sitting close—too close. She looked perfect beside him. The kind of woman any man would be lucky to have.

She looked at them once, twice—before something unfamiliar twisted inside her.

She had no idea why it bothered her.

But it did.

And for the first time, she realized… she didn’t like it.

Rhea snapped her laptop shut, tucking it under her arm as she stood up. "Alright, lovebirds, I’ll leave you two to enjoy your lunch in peace. Don’t worry, I won’t be the third wheel." Her voice was light, teasing—but there was an edge to it that she didn't quite understand herself.

Vivaan frowned slightly. “Rhea, you don’t have to—”

“I insist,” she cut in smoothly, offering a small smile. “I have work to finish, anyway.”

Before Vivaan could say anything else, she was already walking out, the click of her heels fading down the hallway.

Vivaan exhaled, rubbing the back of his neck as he stared at the door she had just exited through. He didn't know why, but the moment she left, the energy in the room shifted. It felt… off.

“She’s quite something, isn’t she?” Sanya commented, pulling out the lunchbox and carefully unpacking it.

Vivaan turned back to her, nodding absentmindedly. “Yeah… she is.”

Sanya tilted her head, watching him with an amused but knowing look. “You keep looking at the door, Vivaan. Are you sure you’re still here?”

Vivaan forced a smirk. “Just thinking about work.”

Sanya let out a soft laugh. “Right. Work.” She handed him a spoon, her expression shifting slightly. “I don’t know why I keep doing this, honestly.”

Vivaan glanced at her. “Doing what?”

“This.” She gestured vaguely between them. “Bringing lunch, showing up, playing the ‘perfect fiancée.’”

Vivaan leaned back on the couch, watching her carefully. “Then why do it?”

Sanya sighed, poking at the rice with her spoon. “I guess it’s because I’m supposed to. Isn’t this what fiancées do?”

Vivaan let out a low chuckle, shaking his head. “You don’t have to pretend with me, Sanya.”

She looked at him then, her brown eyes searching his face. “And you don’t have to pretend with me, either, Vivaan. You don’t even try.”

Vivaan didn't deny it. He couldn’t.

Sanya sighed again, rolling her shoulders as she leaned back slightly. “You know, my mother keeps telling me that a marriage needs effort. That love comes after marriage, not before. But tell me, Vivaan… what happens if love never comes at all?”

Vivaan looked down at his plate, his fingers tightening slightly around the spoon. He didn’t have an answer.

Sanya let out a short laugh, shaking her head. “See? That’s exactly the problem. We both know this engagement isn’t what it should be.”

Vivaan exhaled, rubbing his temple. “Then why do we keep going with it?”

Sanya shrugged. “Because it’s easier than facing the alternative.”

For a moment, there was silence. Not the comfortable kind, but the heavy, suffocating kind that spoke of unsaid truths and hidden regrets.

Vivaan finally pushed his plate aside. “I should get back to work.”

Sanya smiled, but it didn’t quite reach her eyes. “Of course. Work.”

And just like that, the conversation ended—leaving behind an unspoken understanding.

Neither of them were truly in this.

And both of them knew it.

Rhea sat curled up in the plush armchair of her study, a book open on her lap, but she hadn't turned a page in the last twenty minutes. The words blurred together, her mind refusing to focus. Instead, it kept pulling her back—back to the office, back to the sight of Sanya standing beside Vivaan, looking every bit the perfect fiancée.

Her fingers tightened around the edges of the book as she let out a slow breath. Why did it bother me?

It wasn’t like she had any claim over Vivaan. He was engaged. He had a life before he met her, a future already planned. And yet… seeing them together, the effortless way Sanya fit into his life, the way she looked at him like he belonged to her—why did it feel wrong?

She tried to brush it off as annoyance. Maybe it was just irritating to see someone as strong and sharp as Vivaan tied to a relationship that felt so… empty. She saw it in his eyes. He wasn’t happy. But that didn’t explain the twisting feeling in her stomach, the way something inside her had clenched when she watched them have lunch together.

I should be focusing on work. That’s what mattered. That’s what she had fought for.

She forced her gaze back to the book, trying to shake off the thoughts, but they refused to leave.

It wasn’t jealousy. It couldn’t be.

But if it wasn’t… then what was it?

And why did she feel like she had just lost something she never even had?
