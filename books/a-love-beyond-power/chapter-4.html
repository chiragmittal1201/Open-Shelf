Chapter 4

The Disaster & Humiliation

The campaign launched on a Monday morning. Within hours, Kapoor Fashion’s social media engagement skyrocketed. People were intrigued by the behind-the-scenes approach—the raw footage of designers sketching late at night, the candid moments of seamstresses adjusting fabrics, and, most notably, Rhea Kapoor herself, sleeves rolled up, directing a shoot at 2 AM.

The reactions were overwhelmingly positive.

"It’s working," Rhea whispered, almost to herself, as she watched the numbers climb.

Vivaan leaned against the conference table, grinning. "Told you. People don’t connect with perfection, they connect with passion. And right now, they love what they see."

For the first time in a long while, Rhea felt light. The tension that had lived in her shoulders for months had finally lifted. She turned to Vivaan, a rare, genuine smile tugging at her lips. "Maybe you’re not as annoying as I thought."

Vivaan placed a hand over his chest in mock offense. "Coming from you, that’s basically a love confession."

She rolled her eyes, but the usual sharpness in her tone wasn’t there. Instead, she picked up a champagne flute from the celebratory tray that had just been brought in. "To Kapoor Fashion’s bold new era."

Vivaan clinked his glass against hers. "To proving the world wrong."

As she took a sip, her phone buzzed. A message from a board member: This is incredible. You might’ve just changed the game.

Her grip on the glass tightened. I did it.

She met Vivaan’s gaze, and for the first time, there was no doubt. She had trusted him. He had delivered.

But success was a fragile thing. And within 24 hours, everything crumbled.

The first sign of trouble was subtle. A few critical comments trickled into the flood of praise.

Is this supposed to make me want to buy their clothes? Looks like a mess behind the scenes.

Then, a screenshot circulated—a blurred-out segment of the campaign footage where Rhea, mid-discussion, had frowned and muttered, "This entire thing is a mess. We’re running out of time, and everything looks terrible."

Someone cut the clip, stripped it of context, and posted it online.

The headline read: ‘Even the CEO Doesn’t Believe in Kapoor Fashion Anymore’

The video spread like wildfire. Suddenly, people weren’t praising Kapoor Fashion’s authenticity; they were mocking it. Media outlets picked it up, investors panicked, and customers began questioning the brand’s stability.

#KapoorFashionFail started trending.

News anchors dissected the video, calling it a "PR blunder of epic proportions."

Board members flooded Rhea’s inbox, demanding explanations. Investors pulled their funding. Competitors released statements emphasizing their "confidence and excellence," subtly shading Kapoor Fashion in the process.

Rhea watched it all unfold in stunned silence.

Vivaan, standing beside her in her office, ran a hand through his hair. "Rhea, this is just a social media blow-up. It’ll die down. We can spin it—"

"No," she cut him off, her voice eerily calm. "It’s over."

Vivaan frowned. "We just need to—"

"I trusted you!" she snapped, turning to face him, eyes blazing. "I trusted this campaign. I trusted my instincts, and look what happened!"

Vivaan took a step back, guilt settling deep in his chest. "Rhea, you know this wasn’t intentional. We—"

The office door burst open.

And there stood Dilip Kapoor.

The room turned cold the second Dilip entered. His sharp gaze swept over Rhea, then landed on Vivaan with pure disdain.

"Get out," he said, voice low but commanding.

Vivaan clenched his fists but held his ground. "Sir, if we just—"

"I said, get out. I have no business with a small-time photographer who thought he could play corporate genius."

The words hit harder than Vivaan expected.

He had heard insults before. He had been dismissed, underestimated. But this was different. This was in front of Rhea. In front of the woman who had trusted him, worked with him, shared late nights and inside jokes. And she… she said nothing.

He turned to her, waiting for some kind of reaction—a word, a glance, anything to show that she hadn’t completely abandoned him.

But she just stood there. Silent.

A sharp, hollow ache settled in his chest. His fingers curled at his sides, but what was the point of fighting when the person he had fought alongside was now a stranger?

He swallowed hard, forcing himself to nod once, stiffly, before walking past Dilip and out of the office.

He didn’t stop walking. Not through the hallways, not down the elevator, not even when the cold night air hit his face.

With every step, the weight in his chest grew heavier. He wasn’t just leaving Kapoor Fashion.

He was leaving the only thing that had ever made him feel like he belonged.

And Rhea… she didn’t stop him.

Dilip turned back to Rhea, his expression unreadable. "You are no longer fit to lead this company. You will resign by the end of the day."

Rhea swallowed hard, but she didn’t argue. She couldn’t. Because wasn’t this exactly what she had feared? That she would fail? That she would prove him right?

Dilip exhaled sharply, shaking his head. "If this company weren’t your father’s legacy, I would have never given you this position. You were never fit to lead, Rhea. And today, you proved me right."

His words cut deeper than she expected. She had spent years fighting to prove herself, to show that she was more than just the daughter of Aditya Kapoor. That she was capable, strong. But standing there, listening to the man who had doubted her from the beginning, she felt like that lost girl again—the one who had always been told she wasn’t enough.

"You are an emotional fool," Dilip continued, his voice like a hammer against glass. "Your father believed in you, but I knew better. And now, the whole world sees what I’ve always known—you’re just not capable."

Rhea’s breath hitched, but she forced herself to keep her expression neutral. She wanted to scream, to fight, to defend herself.

But what was there left to say?

Before she could react, a sudden commotion outside her office caught her attention. Through the glass doors, she could see a crowd of reporters gathered in the lobby, their microphones raised like weapons. Security struggled to keep them at bay.

A journalist’s voice rang through the chaos. "Ms. Kapoor, do you have a statement regarding the company’s downfall?"

Another one pushed forward. "Do you think your emotional leadership is what led to Kapoor Fashion’s PR disaster?"

"Is it true that investors are demanding your resignation?"

The questions hit her like gunfire, each one echoing her grandfather’s words.

She took a step back. Her breath was shallow. The walls felt like they were closing in.

She didn’t resign. But she disappeared.

The next morning, her office was empty.

No messages. No calls. No explanation.

She was just… gone.

Days passed. Vivaan kept expecting her to return. Expecting to hear from her.

But she never called.

The humiliation of Dilip’s words still lingered, but something else hurt more—the realization that Rhea had let him walk away. That she had chosen silence instead of fighting back.

It wasn’t just a professional failure. It felt like something deeper had shattered.

Vivaan had always known Kapoor Fashion wasn’t his home—it wasn’t his world. But with Rhea, for the first time, he had felt like he belonged, like he was building something real. And now, that was gone. Torn apart in a matter of minutes.

He replayed the moment in his mind—Rhea standing there, saying nothing as Dilip cut him down. He had expected anger, frustration, and maybe even regret. But silence? That was what hurt the most.

Had she meant nothing of what she had said before? The trust, the partnership? Had he been a fool to believe he was more than just another hire?

He sat in his empty apartment, staring at the untouched photos on his laptop. Pictures from the campaign. Shots of Rhea, sleeves rolled up, hair slightly messy, directing a set with the kind of focus that made the world around her disappear.

He closed the laptop with a quiet sigh. It didn’t matter anymore.

He had given his all to something that had crumbled beneath him.

And Rhea Kapoor—she had left him to fall alone.

He had promised her they would make this campaign work.

But maybe some promises were meant to be broken.

And maybe some people were never meant to stay.
